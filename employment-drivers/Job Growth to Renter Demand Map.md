# Where Is the Job Growth Coming From — and How It Becomes Renter Demand at Seasons at Meridian
*Prepared August 14, 2026. The transmission map: sector-level hard-count job growth (QCEW, Q4-2025 vs Q4-2024 — the latest tax-record data) → named employers and projects → wages → qualification math at Seasons' rents → verified capture in the actual tenant base (`tenant-base/` workbooks) → quantified renter-household demand. Sources cited per layer; machine-readable sector data in `appendix/qcew_full_sectors.json`.*

**The one-sentence answer: two-thirds of Boise's job growth is healthcare and construction; the rest is government, business services, and the consumer/logistics economy following rooftops — while manufacturing and the white-collar office economy shrink.** Micron's fingerprint today is inside the construction line, not manufacturing (fab operations hiring starts mid-2027).

## Layer 2 — The employers behind each growing line (named, dated, sourced)

**Healthcare (+4,161; the demand engine):**
- **St. Luke's** — Idaho's largest employer, growing: $1.1B Boise tower (staffing toward 2029–30 opening), the 330K SF consolidated service center *opened in Meridian 2025* (supply-chain/pharmacy jobs), and the Meridian hospital (Idaho's busiest ER, 1.3 mi from Seasons). *[Apps B, C]*
- **Saint Alphonsus** — $450M program: Nampa tower (+64 beds, 2028) and the **North Meridian Health Plaza** (~80K SF, 2028) — staffing ramps ahead of openings. *[App B]*
- **Ambulatory buildout** — Primary Health to 22 valley clinics incl. Idaho's first pediatric urgent care (Meridian); senior/behavioral operators (Cascadia); medical office absorption across the Eagle corridor. *[Apps B, F]*
- Corroboration: SW Idaho hospital job postings **+44% YoY**, RNs the top-posted occupation. *[App H]*

**Construction (+2,159; highest-wage volume sector at $94K):**
- Micron ID1 fit-out & ID2 site prep (~4,500 construction jobs at peak), St. Luke's tower, the $700M airport program, Meta Kuna (~1,400 peak) + Diode, the District at Ten Mile, hospitals, and ACHD/ITD roadwork (SH-16, Linder overpass, Ustick). *[Apps B, H; District dossier]*
- Note the composition: this is *commercial/mega-project* construction labor, much of it high-wage trades (tenant roster shows IBEW electricians at $118K).

**Government (+1,946 state+local through Q4-25):** Boise State (record 28,519 enrollment), West Ada School District (Idaho's largest, HQ in Meridian), Idaho State Police HQ (Meridian, 700+), city/county services scaling with population. 2026 state cuts will dent this line. *[Apps C, H]*

**Logistics/trade/consumer (+1,557 across wholesale, retail, T&W, food):** Amazon Nampa (~2,000), **Tractor Supply DC hiring now (500+, Nampa, opens late-26)**, WinCo and In-N-Out distribution, Costco #2 (late-26), the retail wave following rooftops (Trader Joe's, Scheels 400–500, coming Target/Life Time 2027). *[Apps B, D]*

**And the shrinking column matters for who *isn't* the demand:** HP (exit by 2027), Albertsons corporate, Clearwater/Kount/Cradlepoint, federal agencies — high-income office jobs, W-Boise-skewed. This is why the metro's growth is *workforce-shaped, not luxury-shaped* — which happens to match Seasons' product.

## Layer 4 — The transmission mechanics: wages → qualified households at Seasons' rents

Seasons' qualification hurdle (3.0× screen): **~$61.7K household income at the contract median ($1,714/mo); ~$68.3K at market ($1,897)**. How each growing sector clears it:

| Growth sector | Ada avg wage | Single earner qualifies? | How it actually leases at Seasons (tenant-file evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction | $93,860 | **Yes, solo** | 13.0% of employed tenants; trades pairs common; IBEW at $118K |
| Wholesale/mgmt | $107K–206K | Yes, solo | Small counts — these skew to ownership |
| Healthcare | $66,560 | **Yes at contract; borderline at market** | **#1 tenant industry (19.3%)**, median $55.2K → leases as duals/roommates; St. Luke's + Saint Al's = 32 residents |
| Admin services | $60,736 | Borderline | Part of the prof-services 8.4% tenant share |
| Transport/logistics | $60,320 | Borderline | 6.1% of tenants (Amazon 7) |
| State/local gov't | $54,860–59,748 | No — needs 2nd earner | 7.5% of tenants at median $69.3K (teachers/troopers as duals) |
| Retail | $50,180 | No — roommate math | 4.1% of tenants |
| Food service | $26,780 | No — **two-earner only** | 9.2% of tenants at median $33.4K — the roommate cohort |

**The roommate/dual-earner structure is the conversion mechanism.** Half of Seasons' households have 2+ earners; 34% are roommate households; the median *personal* income is only $58.0K but the median *household* is $80–89K. Two $50K healthcare/government/logistics paychecks make a $100K household at 4.9× coverage. That is precisely how a metro whose growth is concentrated in $27K–$67K-wage sectors produces a tenant base with 79% of units ≥3× rent. It also explains the age profile: the 18–29 cohort (51% of occupants) is the roommate-forming cohort. *[tenant-base/Demographic Analysis: HH Composition, Income; Demographics_Comparison: Income & Coverage]*

**Micron, dimensioned properly:** Seasons holds only 9 Micron-direct residents (2.0%) versus Canyon Ridge's 89 — the fab is 11 miles east and its workforce sorts toward SE Boise (distance decay is visible in the two rent rolls). Micron reaches Seasons through three channels, in order of current size: (1) the **construction line** (fab subs are part of the +2,159, though Seasons captures the civilian construction economy more than fab subs — 12 T2 residents); (2) the **income multiplier** (Micron payroll → healthcare/retail/services jobs metro-wide — i.e., part of why healthcare is +4,161); (3) from mid-2027, **fab operations** (~2,000 direct; operators/techs at Micron's tenant-observed $59–78K fit Seasons' band and the I-84 commute is ~20 min — expect capture to rise from 2% but stay far below CR's). Seasons is not a Micron bet; Micron is upside to a base case that doesn't need it. *[Demographics_Comparison, Tier Summary; App C]*

## Layer 5 — Quantifying the translation: growth → renter households → Seasons

Two demand channels (overlapping — do not sum):

**Channel A — Employment:** +9,196 metro jobs/yr (hard count) ÷ ~1.6 earners per renter household → ~5,700 households supported; at a 35–45% renter propensity for the growth-sector age/wage mix (healthcare/construction/food median tenant ages 30–35) → **~2,000–2,600 renter households/yr**.

**Channel B — Migration:** ~+11.6–12.4K net people/yr (Placer, client-confirmed level range) ÷ ~2.2 persons/household → ~5,300 households; new arrivals rent first at high rates (60%+) → **~3,200–3,800 renter households/yr**.

Against that: metro completions collapsed to ~1,062 units (2025, −64%) with ~600–1,800 under construction (2–3.5% of inventory). **Renter-household formation of roughly 2,500–3,500/yr against ~1,000/yr of new supply is the demand-supply imbalance in one line** — and it reconciles with the observed facts (absorption ~2,500+/yr at 94.3% occupancy while measured payrolls printed ~0: the households kept arriving even when the survey couldn't see the jobs).

**At the property level:** ZIP 83642 alone absorbed **+2,417 net migrants YE Jun-26 (~1,100 households)** — roughly seven times Seasons' annual turn volume (~150 units) forming in the property's own ZIP, before counting the rest of the commute shed. And the intra-metro flow is rotating *toward* the subject (north Meridian −511 while 83642 accelerated +1,077). *[App K; tenant-base/First Turn Analysis for turn volume]*

## The map in one paragraph

Boise's job growth is coming from **healthcare (45% of it) and construction (23%)** — verified in tax records, not surveys — plus government, business services, and a consumer/logistics economy chasing the metro's top-5-in-the-nation migration. Those sectors pay $27K–$94K: too little for today's $550K starter home at 6–7% rates, exactly right for a $1,700–1,900 apartment split between two earners. Seasons sits at the interchange those workers commute through, in the ZIP where the migration is landing hardest, and its rent roll already *is* this map — healthcare #1, construction #2, the corridor's anchors by name, at a $80–89K household income built from $58K individual paychecks paired up. The growth translates to roughly 2,500–3,500 new renter households a year metro-wide against ~1,000 units of new supply; ~1,100 of those households formed in the subject's own ZIP last year. Micron is the accelerant arriving mid-2027 — not the engine, which is exactly what a diversified underwrite wants.
