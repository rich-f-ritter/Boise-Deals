# Appendix F — Eagle Rd/I-84 Node Research Stream (full sourced output)
*Research stream 5. Researched Aug 13, 2026. Synthesized in `../Location Premium - Eagle Rd x I-84.md`; this file preserves the full sourced detail.*

**Bottom line up front:** The Eagle/I-84 interchange is demonstrably the Treasure Valley's highest-traffic, highest-employment, most built-out suburban node — Idaho's busiest arterial crossing Idaho's busiest freeway segment — and the apartment pipeline *at this specific node* is near zero while Ten Mile has ~3,000 units approved. That is a genuine, durable location premium. But the premium argument has real cracks (Blue Cross leaving, Scentsy shrinking, ~21% office vacancy in south Meridian, growth gravity pulling west), and paying **at or above replacement cost** means paying above where every observable Boise-metro trade has cleared ($165k–$222k/unit in 2024–25 vs. ~$210k–$270k/unit to build).

---

## 1. Traffic & Centrality

- **Eagle Rd (SH-55) is Idaho's busiest non-interstate highway: ~60,000 vehicles/day, a volume that has doubled since 2000** (ITD, 2024–2026 variable-speed-limit materials). Sources: [ITD news release](https://itd.idaho.gov/news/itd-activates-variable-speed-limit-signs-on-eagle-road/), [KIVI](https://www.kivitv.com/news/local-news/in-your-neighborhood/meridian/eagle-roads-long-awaited-variable-speed-signs-one-step-closer-to-operation).
- **Just north of I-84 — directly in front of the subject — ACHD counted >64,000 trips/day (2018)**, the heaviest segment of the corridor; ~49,000/day up by The Village. COMPASS projects **+14,000/day by 2040**. Source: [Idaho Press](https://www.idahopress.com/meridian/news/planning_and_construction/officials-estimate-14k-more-cars-on-eagle-road-daily-by-2040/article_6ec60b84-e527-57a7-a02b-8d5e1e19dbb0.html).
- **I-84 is Idaho's busiest roadway, with >147,000 vpd (2024) between the Eagle Rd interchange and the Flying Wye**. Source: [TrafficVision summary of ITD data](https://trafficvision.live/blog/boise-id-traffic-cameras) (secondhand recap — treat precise figure as approximate); corridor context: [COMPASS I-84 Corridor Operations Plan](https://compassidaho.org/wp-content/uploads/Appendix_COMPASS_I-84_CorridorOperationsPlan_2022-06-6.pdf).
- **COMPASS's 2024 Congestion Management System Report identifies the valley's worst congestion as: Chinden (US-20/26), SH-55 Eagle Rd, and I-84's high-volume interchanges** — two of the three intersect at the subject's corner. Source: [COMPASS 2024 CMS Report](https://compassidaho.org/wp-content/uploads/09-2025_2024TreasureValleyAnnualCongestionManagementSystemReport.pdf).
- **Spine role:** SH-55 is the only continuous high-capacity north–south state highway through east Meridian, linking I-84 to SH-44/Eagle and all of north Ada County ([Wikipedia SH-55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_State_Highway_55)). ITD's alternate north–south route has sat as an *unfunded study* since 2006 ([BoiseDev](https://boisedev.com/news/2023/03/29/eagle-highway-study-55-chinden/)) — no relief valve exists or is funded.
- **20-minute reach (ESTIMATE — no published isochrone):** Eagle/I-84 sits ~2 mi from the Boise city limit, ~9 min from the Boise Bench via I-84. A 20-min off-peak drive plausibly reaches most of Ada County (~560K people). Ten Mile and Meridian Rd trade Boise east-side reach for Nampa/Canyon reach. Directionally, Eagle/I-84 maximizes *Ada County* (higher-income) access. Analyst estimate from geography, not a COMPASS-published stat.

## 3. Office/Commercial Health at the Node — Honest Assessment

- **Metro office is healthy:** Boise MSA vacancy 6.6% (multitenant 9.8%), record asking rents ($22.00 overall / $25.50 Class A), sublease down 25% YoY; top Eagle/Meridian projects asking **$45–$55/SF** — highest in the metro. [TOK Q1 2026 Office Trends](https://tokcommercial.com/resources/market-data/boise-msa-office-market-trends-q1-2026/).
- **But the subject's immediate submarket is the soft spot:** Meridian city office vacancy averaged **17.88% in 2025**; **Meridian-South (Silverstone/El Dorado side) was the metro's worst at ~21.0%** vs Meridian-North 13.6% (Yardi/CommercialCafe — different denominator than TOK). [CommercialCafe](https://www.commercialcafe.com/office-market-trends/us/id/meridian/).
- Silverstone backfill inventory: 27,317 SF contiguous at Silverstone Plaza; a listed "former call center"; multiple availabilities ([LoopNet](https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/3405-E-Overland-Rd-Meridian-ID/4159641/), [CBRE](https://www.cbre.com/properties/properties-for-lease/office/details/US-SMPL-194857/silverstone-plaza-office-condo-3405-east-overland-road-meridian-id-83642)).
- **Tenant bleed is real:** Paylocity → Ten Mile (2018); **Blue Cross leaving** ([BoiseDev](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/12/03/blue-cross-says-it-will-leave-large-meridian-campus/)); Scentsy ~40% off peak ([East Idaho News](https://www.eastidahonews.com/2026/03/scented-wax-giant-scentsy-announces-layoffs-at-meridian-headquarters/)). (Bodybuilding.com's Silverstone exit could not be independently confirmed in this pass — treat as unverified.) Offsets: ICCU doubled down (second 150K SF building); Ahlquist starting ~200K SF more at Eagle View 2026. **Net read: the node's newest product (Eagle View) is winning; the 1998–2008 vintage stock is where the vacancy lives.** Second-order for an apartment owner — backfill of cheap office still puts workers near the subject.

## 6. Replacement Cost Context (2025–26)

- **Build cost:** national garden/wrap benchmark **$210K–$270K/unit all-in** ([Multifamily.loans 2026 guide](https://www.multifamily.loans/apartment-finance-blog/multifamily-construction-costs-an-investor-guide/)); Boise cost index ~0.88 ([CountBricks](https://www.countbricks.com/cities/construction-costs-boise)) → **ESTIMATE ~$185K–$240K/unit hard+soft, ~$220K–$280K with land** (labor +~4%/yr).
- **Where trades clear (Lee & Associates/CoStar Q2 2025):** metro average **~$200,000/unit**, caps ~4.4–5.9%; comps: **Ridgecrest Commons, Nampa — 172 units @ $165,000/unit; Telluride, Boise — 102 @ $221,667; The Landon, Boise — 76 @ $200,000** ([Lee Q2 2025 Boise Multifamily PDF](https://www.lee-associates.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-Q2-Boise-ID-Multifamily.pdf)). Q2 2025 median 5+ unit sales: $211,160/unit ([WeKnowBoise](https://www.weknowboise.com/rental-market-vacancy-rates.php)).
- **Trades below replacement → pipeline collapse:** **597 units under construction metro-wide Q4 2025** (CBRE) vs ~5,000+ in 2022; occupancy 94.3%; effective rent $1,637 ([CBRE Q4 2025 Figures](https://www.cbre.com/insights/figures/boise-multifamily-figures-q4-2025)); UC inventory −55% YoY, completions −65% ([MMG 2025 forecast](https://mmgrea.com/2025-boise-forecast/)); absorption ~2,550–2,864/yr (Lee, MMG); Q2 2026 stabilization continuing ([CoStar headline](https://www.costar.com/article/890046601/multifamily-rents-gain-traction-in-boise-idaho), paywalled).

## 7. Interchange/Corridor Investment

- **Past:** I-84 widened to 4 lanes each way Garrity→Meridian (GARVEE, 2009); Eagle Rd WB off-ramp lengthened 1,600 ft with dual-dual turn lanes; Ten Mile interchange 2011 ([FHWA](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/ipd/p3/toolkit/usdot/sep15/sep15_idaho/garvee_report.aspx)).
- **Programmed:** I-84 EB aux lane Meridian IC→Eagle IC, second EB on-ramp lane at Meridian, **second SB off-ramp lane at Eagle** — NEPA cleared June 2025, **$14.8M funded FY2032** ([ITD project](https://itd.idaho.gov/project/i-84-meridian-interchange-to-eagle-interchange/); [ITD staff update Oct 2025](https://mccmeetingspublic.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/meridianid-meet-e8a951d3d58c4ecd8ea63fed8dd17f11/ITEM-Attachment-001-bc7f436dd0534dca8e5aea1eee9068c0.pdf)).
- **Eagle Rd:** ITD response is management, not capacity — variable speed limits (55→45 peaks) Franklin→Chinden, 2-yr pilot from mid-2026; 2023 safety review recommended no widening. ACHD widened Eagle Rd Amity→Lake Hazel (done late 2025) ([BoiseDev](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/08/19/eagle-road-closure-meridian/)).
- **Caveat:** marquee dollars — SH-16 freeway, I-84 SYSTEM/Franklin interchanges, ChindenWest, Linder overpass — flow to the **western** valley ([ITD Oct 2025 update](https://mccmeetingspublic.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/meridianid-meet-e8a951d3d58c4ecd8ea63fed8dd17f11/ITEM-Attachment-001-bc7f436dd0534dca8e5aea1eee9068c0.pdf)).

## 8. Verdict — Evidence For / Against

*(Reproduced in synthesized form in `../Location Premium - Eagle Rd x I-84.md` §5–6.)*

**FOR:** (1) unmatched centrality — Idaho's busiest arterial × busiest freeway segment; (2) deep employment base at the front door (est. 15–25K corridor jobs) vs. a fraction at Ten Mile; (3) supply moat — ~1,100–1,200 existing units, one contested proposal, no MF-ready land left vs ~3,000 approved at Ten Mile; (4) metro's highest office rents on the corridor's new product; capital still building here; (5) metro pipeline collapse + 2,500+/yr absorption; (6) funded interchange investment.

**AGAINST:** (1) paying above where the whole market clears ($165–222K/unit trades vs ~$230–280K+ price) forfeits the cycle's margin of safety; (2) node employment cracks — Blue Cross selling, Scentsy −40% from peak, Meridian-South office ~21% vacant; (3) congestion is the premium's shadow (COMPASS top-3 congestion; management-not-capacity response); (4) center of gravity drifting west (SH-16, SYSTEM interchange, ChindenWest, Linder); (5) the moat is metro-porous — Ten Mile's ~3,000 units are 6 minutes down the same freeway.

**Net:** the premium is real — worth a modest rent/exit-cap advantage, not an unlimited one. At/above replacement prices it in full and leaves the return dependent on the metro supply trough persisting.
