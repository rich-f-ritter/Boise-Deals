# Appendix G — Why US Employment Growth Stalled in 2025–26 (research stream, full sourced output)
*Research stream 6. August 13, 2026. Data as of the July 2026 Employment Situation (Aug 7, 2026) and June 2026 JOLTS (Aug 4, 2026). Feeds §3 of "The Boise Slowdown" research paper.*

## 1. Anatomy of the National Stall

**2025 (post-benchmark):** The final March 2025 benchmark (published Feb 11, 2026) revised 2025 job gains down by **403,000**, leaving calendar-2025 payroll growth at **+181,000 for the entire year** (~15K/month) vs +1.46M in 2024 (Indeed Hiring Lab; KPMG). Pre-benchmark 2025 prints: Sep +119K (released Nov 20, 2025 after the 43-day shutdown), Oct −105K (revised −173K), Nov +64K, Dec +50K.

**2026:** Jan +130K (UR 4.3%); Feb −92K → revised −133K (healthcare strike contributed); Mar +178K → +214K; Apr +115K → +148K; May +129K → +63K; Jun +57K → +20K; **Jul −23K**. Trailing-12-month average as of July 2026: **+34K/month**; persistent downward revisions (May–Jun revised −103K at the July release). July 2026: UR 4.1%; private +30K, government −53K (−50K local-gov education); AHE +3.2% YoY, slowest since May 2021 (BLS).

**Sector concentration — a healthcare-only expansion:** in the 12 months to June 2026, total nonfarm rose +506K, of which health care & social assistance was ~437.5K — **roughly 86%** (BLS TED; Revelio). Healthcare itself is decelerating (>80K/mo 2023 → <60K/mo 2025–26, Yale Budget Lab). Shedding: federal government, manufacturing (3rd straight negative year), retail, information, and in 2026 leisure & hospitality (−61K June) and local-gov education. Private diffusion index: 9 of 12 months of 2025 below 50 (annual avg 48.2, lowest since 2003).

**Low-hire, low-fire (JOLTS June 2026):** openings 7.4M; openings-per-unemployed ~1.0; hires rate 3.4% (touched **3.1% in Feb 2026 — matching the April 2020 COVID low**); quits 2.0% (≤2.0% for 8 straight months); layoffs 1.1%. Cleveland Fed (EC 26-17): hiring at levels historically associated with weak economies. Powell (Jackson Hole, Aug 22, 2025): a "**curious kind of balance**" from "a marked slowing in both the supply of and demand for workers," attributed "much more" to immigration.

## 2. Causes, Quantified

### 2a. Immigration collapse — the largest supply-side factor
- Net migration: +2.2M (2024) → **negative in 2025 for the first time in at least half a century** per AEI-Brookings (−295K to −10K); CBO says ~+400K; 2026 projected between −925K and +185K. Census vintage-2025: national growth +1.78M with net international migration −54% to 1.3M.
- **Dallas Fed (Mar 31, 2026):** net unauthorized migration turned negative Feb 2025, averaging **−55K/month in H2 2025**, projected −89K/month by July 2026.
- **Break-even payroll growth:** Dallas Fed — from ~250K/mo (2023) to ~10K/mo (Jul 2025) and **~−3K/mo average Aug–Dec 2025**; St. Louis Fed 2026 range 15K–87K; Blue Chip ~55K; KC Fed and Fed Board concur near-zero. Goldman: "much but not all" of the deceleration is supply; <70K/mo suffices to hold UR steady in 2026.

### 2b. Tariffs
Average effective rate ~14.3% pre-SCOTUS (highest since 1939); **IEEPA tariffs struck down Feb 20, 2026**; replacement Section 122 (10%) tariffs → ~10.5–11.8%. Manufacturing lost ~59–89K jobs from Apr 2025 through end-2025 (CAP; Cato/AEI dispute attribution — contested); manufacturing construction spending −14% Dec'24–Dec'25; households paid ~$1,700 in tariffs Feb'25–Jan'26. Tariffs pushed the June 2026 SEP inflation projection to 3.6%, blocking rate relief.

### 2c. Federal workforce & spending
Federal civilian workforce **−10.3% (−238K) in 2025** (Pew); GAO: −256K (−11%) across 22 agencies Dec'24–Jan'26; ~10,400 RIF removals + Deferred Resignation (~$4.5B payouts; ~$71B total fiscal cost est.). Indirect: proposed FY2026 cuts ~40% NIH, ~57% NSF; ~70K jobs at risk from NIH cuts alone; university layoffs. Geographic signature: **Washington DC metro −100.5K (−3.0%) YoY through May 2026 — the largest metro decline in the country.**

### 2d. AI and entry-level hiring — CONTESTED
Stanford/ADP ("Canaries in the Coal Mine"): ~16% relative employment decline for workers 22–25 in most-AI-exposed occupations since late 2022, deepening through 2026. New-grad UR ~5.6%; tech hiring demand −33% from pre-2022 norms. Skeptics: Yale Budget Lab finds no aggregate AI-unemployment relationship; St. Louis Fed says young-worker struggles are "(Still) the Business Cycle." Treat as plausible contributor to the white-collar freeze with no consensus aggregate magnitude.

### 2e. Rates & housing
Fed cut 3× in 2025 to 3.50–3.75%, then held through all of 2026 to date (June 2026 meeting the first under new Chair Kevin Warsh). June 2026 SEP: PCE 3.6%, GDP 2.2%, **9 of 18 dots favored at least one 2026 hike**. Mortgage rates ≥6%; May 2026 starts 1.177M SAAR (lowest since May 2020); NAHB projects MF starts −5% in 2026. Paradox: national construction employment held up better than starts partly because enforcement shrank the construction labor force as demand fell.

### 2f. Consumer softness
L&H +0.7% YoY nationally (mid-2026) with −61K in June 2026 despite the World Cup; retail −19K July; AHE +3.2% caps spending power; international inbound travel a continuing drag (U.S. Travel).

## 3. The Measurement Question
- **March 2025 preliminary benchmark: −911K (−0.6%)** — the largest on record; final benchmark confirmed (2025 revised down 403K). Pre-revision 2024–25 monthly gains overstated ~76K/month.
- **Birth-death reform (Jan 2026):** quarterly updates with current sample info; reformed forecasts 185K lower over 7 months — direct evidence the model overstated business-formation jobs during the immigration reversal.
- **QCEW vs CES nationally:** QCEW through Q4 2025 suggests employment peaked ~Nov 2025 and declined into early 2026 — i.e., nationally QCEW points to MORE weakness, not less (Econbrowser, Jun 2, 2026). The **March 2026 preliminary benchmark lands Aug 28, 2026** — risk of another downward national revision.
- Caveats: QCEW denominator affected by unauthorized workers exiting covered employment; Chicago Fed argues population controls make the UR more reliable than payroll growth right now.
- **Bottom line: nationally, the 2025 weakness was initially understated by CES and the 2026 stall is mostly real.** (Note the asymmetry with Boise: locally, QCEW runs far ABOVE CES — see research paper §2.)

## 4. Mapping National Forces onto Boise
- National forces present in Boise: immigration/labor supply (Idaho labor force fell in spring 2026; IDOL cites immigration/deportation policy constraining ag, construction, L&H); consumer softness (L&H largest negative); government (Boise −1.3% mirrors federal/local-ed contraction; Idaho is a federal-lands state); rates (metro median ~$534.7K, +2.9% YoY, ~1.5 months supply).
- Local amplifier: Boise's prior +2.5–3% growth was in-migration-dependent; when both international and domestic flows shrink (Census: national domestic-mobility narrowing in nearly all metros), an importer of labor decelerates mechanically at higher amplitude.
- Local offset: Micron ($50B+, ~4,500 construction jobs, first fab "blowdown" spring 2026) — why Boise construction outgrew the nation.
- Cross-metro: in the year to May 2026, only **8 of 387 metros** had statistically significant employment increases; 4 declined (DC −3.0%, Portland −2.8%); SLC +2.1% shows Mountain West metros can still outgrow.
- Rough attribution: of Boise's ~2.1pp T3M deceleration, roughly half matches the national deceleration; the excess ~1pp is compression of the migration-dependent growth premium, concentrated in L&H and government, partially offset by Micron construction.

## 5. Forward View (2027)
- Break-even "new normal": ~0–90K/month consensus range through 2027; CBO has payroll growth slowing again in 2027–28 as fiscal impulse fades; UR ~4.4–4.5%.
- Fed: 3.50–3.75% with a hawkish tilt; markets/NAHB pencil cuts toward ~3.25% if inflation fades post-Section 122 expiry (Jul 24, 2026). Re-acceleration channels: immigration normalization (± ~1.1M workers/yr across scenarios — the biggest swing factor), rate cuts, tariff sunset.
- Interpretation caveat: with break-even near zero, a healthy 2027 market may print only +30–70K/month — judge slack by UR, prime-age EPOP, wages.

## Contested-claims register
1. AI displacement magnitude (Stanford/ADP vs Yale) — contested.
2. Tariff job-loss attribution (CAP −89K vs Cato/AEI ~0) — direction consensus, magnitude contested.
3. 2025 net migration level (Brookings negative vs CBO +400K) — deportation counts and voluntary-exit assumptions.
4. Supply vs demand shares — presented as joint.
5. Boise T3M sensitivity — CES small-area noise; state/area re-benchmark March 2027.
