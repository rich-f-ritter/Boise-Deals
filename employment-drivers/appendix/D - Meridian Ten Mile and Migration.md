# Appendix D — Meridian / Ten Mile Employment & Boise Metro Migration
*Research stream 4 of 4. Prepared August 13, 2026. Subject: 2700 E Overland Rd, SE Meridian (note: subject sits ~2.5 mi east of the Ten Mile/I-84 interchange, in the Overland/Eagle–Silverstone corridor; Ten Mile Crossing is the nearest major master-planned employment node to the west).*

## PART A — MERIDIAN & TEN MILE CORRIDOR EMPLOYMENT

### A1. Ten Mile Crossing (Brighton Corp., NE quadrant of Ten Mile Rd & I-84)

**Scale (confirmed):** 300+ acre master-planned center; **over 2 million SF of commercial space built** as of mid-2026, and Brighton says the development is only **~two-thirds complete** (Idaho Statesman/AOL, July 9, 2026). Earlier planning targeted ~1.3M SF of office alone. Developed by Brighton Corporation (originally with Gardner Company); it anchors Meridian's Ten Mile urban renewal district, which is already generating tax-increment reimbursements.

**Anchor retail (confirmed):** **Scheels** — 240,000+ SF of retail (316,000+ SF including attached storage), opened **April 2024**, **400+ employee-owners**. Largest sporting-goods anchor in Idaho and the traffic engine of the whole node.

**Office/employer roster (confirmed, from Ten Mile Crossing's own directory + press):**
- **Brighton Corporation HQ** — 2929 W. Navigator Dr., 4th floor of an 84,000 SF four-story building it built for itself.
- **Paylocity** (NASDAQ HR-software co.) — 62,000 SF across floors 3-5 at 2775 W. Navigator; relocated from Boise in 2018 explicitly for expansion capacity. Still actively hiring in Meridian as of 2024-2026. (Site-level headcount not published; the space supports roughly 300-450 seats at typical densities — estimate, not a reported figure.)
- **AmeriBen** (employee-benefits TPA) — first tenant (70,000 SF, 2017); **~850 employees company-wide, ~500 in the Ten Mile building**; broke ground on a **second AmeriBen building** at Ten Mile Crossing.
- **Horrocks Engineering** — 2775 W. Navigator Ste. 210.
- **Leidos** (Fortune-500 defense/IT) — three full suites at 2487 W. Navigator.
- Others in the directory: Okland Construction, J-U-B Engineers, KB Home, Guild Mortgage, Berkley North Pacific, Liberty Mutual, Northwestern Mutual, Acrisure, Gallagher, Allen Lund, American Credit Acceptance, Hershey's Sales Co., Zennify, US Bank Home Mortgage, Petra General Contractors, Darigold office, Ocean Network Express (coming).
- **Medical users:** Primary Health clinic, Limetree Labs, Alliance Dental, 20/20 Eyecare, Foothills PT, Functional Medicine of Idaho, LHC Group, Excelsia, WestVet (coming).
- **ICCU** — full-service branch at 85 S. Innovation Ln. **Important nuance:** ICCU's big Meridian *operations campus* is NOT at Ten Mile — it's at **Eagle View Landing (Eagle Rd/I-84)**: a 128,000 SF five-story building (opened 2020) plus a second administration building, housing call center, mortgage, commercial and wealth ops. ICCU is now a **$12.1B-asset institution, ~2,172 employees company-wide, ~672,000 members**, and merged into Arizona (Tucson Old Pueblo CU) effective Feb 1, 2025.
- **"PKG" and "WEST Ahern": not found.** No tenant by either name appears in the Ten Mile Crossing directory or press coverage — treat as unverified/likely confused with other tenants (Paylocity is the confirmed HR-tech tenant; Ahern does not appear).

**Under construction / 2026-27 pipeline (confirmed):**
- **Sentinel Building** — ~100,000 SF, four stories, 2850 W. Navigator; topped out June 2026; **completion early 2027**; Brighton says it has a **committed anchor tenant it has not yet named** (flex office + healthcare layout, 300+ stalls).

**Employment count caution:** the only official figure found is dated — Meridian Development Corp. cites "1,100 employees" in the Ten Mile URD, which clearly predates Scheels/Leidos/Paylocity build-out. Realistic current on-site employment is well above that but no verified 2026 total exists; do not print a hard number.

**District at Ten Mile (WEST side of Ten Mile Rd — separate development, confirmed):** 220-222 acres; **broke ground May 5, 2026**. Committed: **Meridian's first Target (148,000 SF, opening ~May 2027)**, **Life Time Fitness**, hotels, day care, industrial/flex buildings, **~1,800 residential units** (apartments + up to 400 townhomes), and a planned **In-N-Out** (the valley's 4th). This is the single biggest near-term jobs/retail catalyst in the submarket. Note: BVA (Ball Ventures Ahlquist) is a separate developer active elsewhere in Meridian; the master developer branding on this project is "The District at Ten Mile" — verify sponsor attribution before naming in the memo.

### A3. City of Meridian — population & strategy

- **Population:** COMPASS 2026 estimate **~147,300-148,000** (147,340, +3.1% y/y); Census Vintage 2024 put it at 139,740. Meridian is **Idaho's 2nd-largest city** (passed Nampa), up from 117,635 at the 2020 Census — **~24% growth in six years**.
- **Growth rank:** +18.6% 2020-2024, **71st fastest-growing US city** (Census, May 2025 release); repeatedly top-10 nationally in the late 2010s. Growth is decelerating from ~5%/yr (2015-2021) to ~3%/yr — still triple the national rate.
- **Strategy:** City comprehensive plan brand is **"Built for Business, Designed for Living"**; explicit policy of adding employment ("premier" job centers at Ten Mile interchange per the **Ten Mile Interchange Specific Area Plan**) and housing near jobs. AEI's housing playbook estimates Meridian is **short ~2,500 housing units (~4.9% of stock)** — a structural tailwind for rental demand.
- **Jobs-to-housing:** the city has historically been a bedroom community rebalancing toward employment; Ten Mile Crossing, District at Ten Mile, Eagle View Landing and Silverstone are the rebalancing engine. No official 2026 jobs-housing ratio published; frame qualitatively.

### A4. I-84 logistics corridor (Meridian–Nampa)

- **Amazon Nampa fulfillment center** (Franklin/Star Rd, ~8 mi west of subject): 650,000-SF footprint four-story robotics FC opened 2020; announced 1,000 jobs, **actual employment grew to ~2,000+** (Panattoni/company statements). Amazon also runs delivery stations in Nampa and near Boise Airport.
- The Meridian-Nampa I-84 strip continues to add distribution/industrial: District at Ten Mile includes industrial/flex buildings; Star Rd/Franklin Rd interchange area (Nampa side) has become the metro's big-box logistics node (Amazon, Lactalis cheese plant adjacent). Treat as steady blue-collar demand feeding 2-mile-radius rentals.

## PART B — MIGRATION & DEMAND DRIVERS (BOISE MSA)

### B1. Net migration — still positive, below boom peak

- **Ada County population:** 526,690 (2023) → 535,799 (2024) → **+10,916 in the year to July 2025 (~546,700)** — the largest absolute gain in Idaho, and **faster than its 2024 gain** (Census Vintage 2025 via Idaho Dept. of Labor, March 2026 release).
- **Composition:** statewide, **~3 of every 4 new Idaho residents in 2025 came from net in-migration**; natural increase added ~6,900 (births > deaths). Idaho grew **1.4% July 2024-July 2025** (vs 1.5% prior year) — 2nd-4th fastest state depending on measure. Applying the ~75% migration share to Ada's ~11K gain ⇒ roughly **8,000+ net migrants into Ada County in 2025** (derived, not published).
- **Where from:** California is the #1 feeder — **~27,000 Californians moved to Idaho in 2022** (Census state-to-state flows); **11,797 CA driver's licenses surrendered in Idaho in the most recent ITD year, up from 10,682** — and **Ada County is the top landing spot**, followed by Kootenai and Canyon. Washington and Oregon are the other top origins. United Van Lines/Atlas 2025 data still rank **Idaho a top inbound state** (Idaho Business Review, Jan 2026).
- **Honest read:** in-migration is **clearly below the 2020-2022 boom** (when the MSA led the nation and Redfin ranked Boise the #1 in-migration destination relative to size). It has *stabilized* at a strong-but-normalized level, and 2025 actually re-accelerated modestly in Ada County. It has not reversed.

### B2. MSA growth & forecasts

- **Boise MSA (Ada, Canyon, Boise, Gem, Owyhee):** **845,877 (Census 2024 est.)**, +2.1% y/y — **13th fastest-growing US metro**; averaging 10,000+ new residents/yr since 2020. 2026 population is ~860-870K on trend.
- **COMPASS forecasts:** CIM 2050 projects **~1.1 million in Ada+Canyon by 2050** (vs ~800K today); the CIM 2055 planning forecast, accepted Dec 2023, is **1.3 million by 2055**. That's ~10,000-12,000 people/yr for three decades — the planning baseline for every road and school in the valley.

### B3. Remote work & migrant incomes

- Redfin: typical Boise homebuyer income hit **$98,000 in 2021, +24% in two years**, driven by inbound remote workers from coastal metros — the clearest documented income gap between in-migrants and locals. Idaho wages still rank near the bottom of states, so in-migrants continue to out-bid locals.
- Remote/hybrid in-migration continues but at a slower clip post-2022 (national return-to-office pressure); no 2025-specific Boise remote-worker count exists. Frame as: the remote-work channel built a higher-income renter/buyer cohort in 2020-2022 and remains a positive but diminished flow.
- Metro-level driver worth one line: **Micron's multi-billion-dollar Boise fab expansion** (announced 2022, construction/hiring through the late 2020s) underpins high-wage job growth forecasts for the MSA.

### B4. Own-vs-rent gap — the core rental-demand argument

- **Prices (Redfin, 3 mo ending June/July 2026):** Meridian median sale **~$550K**; Boise **~$535K**. Idaho statewide ~$462K (Jan 2026).
- **Rents:** Boise average apartment rent **~$1,650-1,720** (Zillow/RentCafe/Apartment List, 2025-26; +~3% y/y); Meridian median asking ~$1,805.
- **The spread:** at ~6.2-7% mortgage rates, P&I+taxes+insurance on a median Meridian house runs **~$3,300-3,600/mo** (with ~5-10% down) vs ~$1,700-1,800 rent — an **own-vs-rent premium of roughly $1,500-1,900/mo, i.e., renting is ~half the monthly cost of buying**. ATTOM's Rental Affordability Report confirms Boise is a rent-favorable (high price-to-rent) market. Existing owners are locked in at 2.5-4% rates, freezing resale inventory and first-time-buyer pathways alike. This is the strongest structural argument for durable renter demand at the subject.

### B5. Honest negatives (put these in the memo)

1. **Migration has cooled vs. the boom** — Idaho state growth eased 1.5% → 1.4% (2025); net gains are smaller than 2020-2022 and national coverage ("Idaho Migration Wave Slows") is directionally right, even though Ada County's 2025 gain beat 2024.
2. **Affordability strain / local out-migration:** Boise-area prices roughly doubled in a decade while Idaho wages remain near the bottom of states; documented outflows of priced-out locals to Nampa/Caldwell, Twin Falls, and out of state. Redfin outbound data show Boise residents searching Salt Lake City, Coeur d'Alene, and lower-cost metros.
3. **Employer wobbles:** **Scentsy cut 116 Meridian jobs (11%) in April 2025** after earlier restructuring; Bodybuilding.com (Silverstone) shrank years ago; **Target pulled out of the Hawkins Meridian Rd/Tanner Creek project (July 2025)** before landing at District at Ten Mile — retail commitments in this metro do move.
4. **Rent growth is modest, not boom-era:** ~3% y/y in Boise with ~3,000+ units under construction metro-wide moderating pricing power; NARPM vacancy 2.0-3.5% (low, but up from 2024 troughs in some segments); Boise Regional Realtors' Dec 2025 outlook flagged **possible 2026 home-price dip** — good for the rent-vs-own story, bad for exit-comp optimism.
5. **Entitlement risk is real even in pro-growth Meridian** — the In-N-Out Ten Mile/Chinden denial (Sept 2025, 5-1, on traffic) shows rising resident pushback on congestion in Ten Mile corridors.
6. **Data gaps to flag:** no verified 2026 headcounts for Paylocity-Meridian, T-Mobile-Meridian, or total Ten Mile Crossing employment; Sentinel anchor tenant unnamed; the Meridian Rd/I-84 warehouse retailer (possible Costco/Sam's) is **unconfirmed**.
