## 3. Tech Sector — The Honest Picture (2025–2026)

**Net assessment: Boise tech is consolidating, not booming.** Legacy/acquired tech employers (HP, Ericsson/Cradlepoint, Equifax/Kount) are all shrinking Boise headcount; the flagship (Clearwater) is going private under PE ownership; startup activity is modest. Micron is absorbing much of the valley's engineering talent.

- **Clearwater Analytics** (Boise's flagship SaaS, downtown HQ): FY2025 revenue **$731.4M, +62%** (inflated by its 2025 acquisitions of Enfusion and Beacon); ARR $841M. On **Dec 21, 2025 agreed to an $8.4B take-private** by Permira/Warburg Pincus ($24.55/sh). Still hiring in Boise, but PE ownership plus a now-global, acquisition-built footprint creates real long-term HQ-consolidation risk — flag it. ([Permira](https://www.permira.com/news-and-insights/announcements/clearwater-analytics-to-be-acquired-for-84-billion-by-permira-and-warburg-pincus-supported-by-francisco-partners-and-with-participation-from-temasek); [StockTitan 10-K summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/CWAN/10-k-a-clearwater-analytics-holdings-inc-amends-annual-report-e2537930d754.html))
- **Cradlepoint → Ericsson:** Nov 2024 layoffs at the Boise operation (undisclosed count); brand folded into "Ericsson Enterprise Wireless"; Ericsson declined to state any future commitment to Boise. Trajectory is clearly down from the ~700+ local peak. ([BoiseDev](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/11/13/ericsson-boise-layoffs/))
- **Kount → Equifax:** Jan 2025 layoffs — several dozen roles per LinkedIn posts; Kount unit at ~180 employees globally by early 2026. Downtown Boise office retained but smaller. ([BoiseDev](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/01/21/kount-equifax-layoffs/))
- **PetIQ (Eagle):** taken private by Bansk Group **Oct 25, 2024**; Eagle HQ (opened 2021, ~250 HQ employees) retained so far; watch for PE-driven consolidation. ([Stock Analysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/petq/company/); [IBR HQ opening](https://idahobusinessreview.com/2021/07/08/petiq-debuts-new-headquarters-in-eagle/))

Still private, still Boise-based. ([Yahoo/Idaho Statesman](https://www.yahoo.com/news/boise-baby-toy-maker-lays-100000643.html); [Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/Lovevery))

## Bottom Line for Underwriting

1. **Job growth is real but decelerating and narrow.** Boise MSA added ~358 jobs/month avg in 2025 and ~567/month in 2026 YTD — positive but among the weaker stretches since 2020; state unemployment ticked up to 3.7% in mid-2026. ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-jobs-were-added-in-the-us-last-month/metro-area/boise-city-id/); [IBR](https://idahobusinessreview.com/2026/06/24/idaho-unemployment-rate-rises-slightly-to-3-7-percent/))
2. **The non-Micron growth engine is healthcare + logistics + data-center construction**, concentrated in the **western valley (Nampa, Caldwell, Kuna, south Meridian)**: Saint Alphonsus ~$450M, St. Luke's $1.1B, Tractor Supply 500 jobs, Meta/Diode ~$2B+ in Kuna, Costco #2 at Lake Hazel. This favors Canyon County and south-Meridian/Kuna submarket demand.
3. **White-collar office employment is a net negative story**: HP (−1,100 by 2027), Albertsons (several hundred), Ericsson, Equifax, Wells Fargo. Downtown Boise office-worker-driven demand should be underwritten conservatively; Clearwater's take-private adds tail risk.
4. **Data centers are capex-rich but jobs-light** (~100 permanent each) — their apartment-demand value is in the 1,000+ person construction workforces through ~2027–2028, which is transient demand.
5. **Retail anchors (Costco, Scheels, In-N-Out, Trader Joe's, Topgolf) confirm rooftop-driven momentum** but are effects of population growth, not causes; Buc-ee's remains an unfiled rumor.
