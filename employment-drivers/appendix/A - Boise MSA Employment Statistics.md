## 3. Unemployment Rate & Labor Force

**Boise MSA unemployment (NSA, FRED BOIS216URN):** Jun 2026 **3.5%** vs Jun 2025 3.4%; 2026 path: Jan 4.0% → Feb 3.8% → Mar 3.6% → Apr 3.3% → May 3.3% → Jun 3.5%. December readings: 2021 2.4%, 2022 2.4%, 2023 3.1%, 2024 3.3%, 2025 3.2%. Jun 2026 by county (BLS summary): Ada 3.4%, Canyon 3.7%, Gem 3.6%, Boise Co. 3.8% — all well below **US 4.4%**. **Idaho statewide (SA, IDOL):** 3.7% Feb 2026, 3.6% Mar–Apr, 3.7% May–Jun 2026.

**Labor force (FRED BOIS216LFN, MSA, NSA) — the key soft spot:**
- Dec levels: 2021: 415,954 → 2022: 427,417 (+2.8%) → 2023: 442,466 (+3.5%) → 2024: 454,200 (+2.7%) → 2025: 459,335 (**+1.1%** — sharp deceleration)
- 2026: Jan 457,913 → Apr 451,594 → Jun 453,618. **Jun 2026 is −3,064 (−0.7%) below Jun 2025 (456,682)** — the first sustained YoY labor force contraction of the post-COVID era.
- Statewide corroboration: Idaho's labor force fell ~2,350 (−0.2%) in April 2026 (IDOL via KTVB). This suggests in-migration-driven labor supply growth has at least paused; underwrite demand from in-migration more conservatively than 2021–2024 trend.

- **Average weekly wage, Boise metro (QCEW): $1,476 in Q4 2025 vs US $1,569** (94% of US) — BLS Boise Area Economic Summary, Aug 6, 2026.
- **Ada County Q3 2025 (QCEW):** employment 295,400 (+1.7% YoY, ranked 16th nationally among large counties for employment growth); avg weekly wage $1,361, **+4.4% YoY**. **Canyon County:** employment 90,600 (+0.8%); avg weekly wage $1,038, **+4.7% YoY**. Idaho statewide: $1,180, +4.9%; US: $1,459, +4.7%. (BLS County Employment & Wages, Idaho, released 3/18/2026.)
- **Idaho OEWS 2025 (IDOL, July 2026):** statewide average wage $29.41/hr, **+4.7% ($1.31) vs 2024 — 3rd-fastest wage growth in the nation** (behind KY, MT); median $23.06/hr (+3.2%). **Southwestern Idaho region highest in state at $30.76/hr.**
- **Boise MSA OEWS May 2025 (BLS):** all-occupations mean $31.02/hr vs US $33.54 (92.5% of US).
- **Median household income (2024 ACS): Ada County $91,502** (~17.6% above Idaho, 13.3% above US; USAFacts/Census); **Meridian $100,795** (up 2.1% from $98,686 in 2023; DataUSA/ACS). Note: Census QuickFacts shows $91,500 for Ada as a 2020–2024 5-yr figure — 1-yr and 5-yr estimates are nearly identical here.
- IDOL forecasts Idaho total covered wages of $55.5B by Q2 2027 vs $52.6B Q2 2025 (~2.7%/yr nominal — a marked slowdown from 2020–2025 pace).

## 6. Softening vs. Acceleration — Honest Read

**Evidence of cooling (2024–2026):**
- Growth decelerated four consecutive years: +2.7% (2023) → +2.5% (2024) → +2.0% (2025) → ~+0.5% YoY on a 2026 YTD-average basis. **April 2026 printed a negative YoY (−0.3% NSA)** — the first since 2020.
- **MSA labor force is down −0.7% YoY (June 2026)** and Idaho's labor force shrank in early 2026 — a real constraint/demand signal for a market whose story is in-migration.
- Leisure & hospitality −3.0% YoY and government −1.3% YoY (state/federal austerity; Boise is the state capital, so this hits directly). Information −4.4%.
- **Micron construction hiccup:** Micron "unexpectedly permanently discontinued" GC Exyte's contract on the Boise project effective Sept 2, 2025 → 201 permanent layoffs (construction management/engineering) per Idaho WARN coverage (Idaho Statesman via Yahoo Finance).
- Unemployment has drifted up from cycle lows (MSA ~2.4% Dec 2021–22 → 3.3–3.5% now), though it remains ~1 pt below the US.
- IDOL itself calls the market "normalizing" and projects just 1.2%/yr growth — half the 2021–2023 pace.

**Evidence of resilience/acceleration:**
- June 2026 YoY re-accelerated to +1.2% (+5,100), the strongest reading of 2026 and the largest monthly gain among Idaho MSAs; 2026 monthly SA gains (+567/mo) are running ahead of 2025 (+358/mo).
- **Construction +4.8% YoY**, powered by Micron: ID1 hit cleanroom "blowdown" in spring 2026 with fit-out dominant through H2 2026, first wafers targeted 2027 and ramp through 2028; **ID2 is in early site prep with ~$1.2B of CHIPS funding redirected from the delayed NY project to Boise**; project totals cited: 2,000 direct Micron jobs, 4,500 construction jobs, 15,000 indirect (Micron; KIVI; TrendForce/Construction Dive on NY delay).
- Wage growth top-3 in the nation; unemployment still low; Boise growing ~5x the US rate.

**Underwriting takeaway:** this is a mid-cycle cooldown from an overheated base, not a contraction — but the labor-force decline and the leisure/government/information losses mean job-growth-driven rent assumptions should use ~1.0–1.5%/yr, not the 2.5–3.5% of 2021–2024. Micron's 2027–2028 operational ramp is the main identifiable upside catalyst; it is also a single-company, deeply cyclical (memory) risk.
