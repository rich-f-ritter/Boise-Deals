The range came from the Lee & Associates Q2 2025 Boise Multifamily report, whose entire trailing-12-month "Top Sale Transactions" table was three sub-institutional deals:

Lee itself flags the ~$200K average "driven by low sales volume and limited reporting data" (Idaho is a **non-disclosure state**), and its own series shows **Q1 2025 average of $348,020/unit** — quarters with real institutional product triple the average. None of the three comps is institutional-size Ada County Class A.

## 1. Prelude at Paramount, Meridian — full trade history
**Asset:** 4909 N. Elsinore Ave., Meridian (Paramount master plan). 280 units, 21 buildings, garden, avg **1,016 SF**, completed 2018/2019. Developed by **Brighton Corp. + Pacific Partners Residential** (The Pacific Companies). Pool/spa/24-hr fitness/clubhouse/smart-home.
- **Trade #1 — Jan 2022:** Brighton/Pacific Partners sold to **Pacific Development Partners** (LA; first Idaho buy). Brokered by IPA/Marcus & Millichap (Danny Shin, Brock Zylstra, et al.). **Price undisclosed.** Peak-market (Class A caps then ~4% or lower).
- **Trade #2 — closed Dec 30, 2025** (announced Jan 6–13, 2026): **The Milestone Group** acquired Prelude in a 602-unit year-end duo with Bucking Horse (Fort Collins, 322u). IPA (Shin & Zylstra) again; marketed as "stabilized… upside through organic rent growth." **Price undisclosed** in all coverage (Milestone PR, CoStar, Multifamily Dive, REBusinessOnline, Yield PRO, Mile High CRE). Management: Avenue5 → **Greystar** post-close.
- **Pricing verification:** unverifiable publicly — but client's statement (above $222K/unit) is fully consistent: $222K/unit = only ~$219/SF on 1,016 SF units vs ~$280–320/SF replacement. No public source contradicts; no deed price exists (non-disclosure).

## 3. What newer-vintage product clears at
- CoStar/Lee quarterly avg $/unit: Q2'24 $188.8K → Q3'24 $216.9K → Q4'24 $190.7K → **Q1'25 $348.0K** → Q2'25 $200.0K — the whipsaw is a volume artifact.
- **Synthesis: 2020s-vintage, 150+ unit Class A garden in Ada County clears roughly $250K–$340K/unit ($230–300/SF)**, Eagle/I-84 and Ten Mile nodes at the top.
- **~$328K/unit for Seasons (≈$118M, ~$304/SF on 388,767 SF) sits in the upper part of that band — defensible, ≈ replacement-cost parity.** $165–222K describes 2017-and-older, smaller, or Canyon County product. Seasons is being marketed by CBRE (unpriced); Ada assessment $62.8M (~$174K/unit) — Idaho commercial assessments run far below market.

## 4. True institutional replacement cost, 2025–26
- **Seasons itself: "$100 million-plus" for 360 units at Jan-2023 pricing = ≥$278K/unit** (Multifamily Dive, quoting developer). Escalate 3–5%/yr → **~$305–330K/unit today**.
- Dovetail (240u, 2021): $60M construction-only = $250K/unit pre-spike.
- **Village Apartments (336u, 4-story, 2022): $68M SENIOR construction loan = $202K/unit of debt alone**; at 60–65% LTC → ~$300–335K/unit total.
- Roundhouse CEO Casey Lynch (Jul 2023): Fowler-equivalent costs "100% to 125% more" than 2015; redesigning The Avens "so that the project makes economic sense to build."
- Land: Boise land +18% YoY in 2023; Gibson site $80→$160+/SF in 5 yrs; suburban Meridian MF land ~$25–40K/unit at Seasons density. Idaho ~5,000 construction workers short; labor +~4%/yr.
- **Bottom line: Seasons-equivalent today ≈ $300–340K/unit all-in** (hard $200–240K + land $25–40K + soft/financing $60–80K at ~7% construction money). Client's $328K ≈ **~100% of replacement**; the erroneous $165–222K range = **~55–70% of replacement** — a level at which no developer starts, which is exactly what permits show.

## 5. The broken development math (quantified)
- Supply response: MF permits fell two-thirds (596 units Jan–Apr 2023 vs 4,739 in 2022); starts −40% (2024); completions −64% (2025, 1,062 units); **under construction 1,125–1,848 units ≈ 2–3.5% of 50,408-unit inventory**; 2025 first year since 2020 absorption (~1,692u) exceeded deliveries (Lee Q2'25; MMG).
- Rents: metro asking $1,575 (Q2'25); Seasons in-place $1,597 (studio) – $2,529 (3BR), ~$1,950–2,000 blended (~$1.85–2.00/SF). Against ~$320K/unit cost at 6.0–6.5% required YoC (vs ~5.2% market cap): need **~$19.5–21K NOI/unit → ~$2,500–2,700/mo blended — a 25–35% rent gap**.
- Developer quotes: Clay Carley: "The rent you need to get to cover the interest rate on the loan is not in the market today… Much higher rents." Morgan Stonehill's 351-unit Seasons Phase 2 remains in concept design/entitlement.

## 6. Cap-rate context
- TOK: Treasure Valley MF lowest-cap sector at **5.2% (2025)**; Q3→Q4 2024 A/B 4.90%→5.05%.
- CBRE (as cited locally): value-add A ~4.74%, B ~4.92%, C ~5.38%; H2 2025 national survey puts secondary stabilized Class A ~4.75–5.5%+.
- Lee/CoStar 4.4% (Q2'25) flagged "not indicative" (thin data). At $328K/unit, realistic Seasons NOI (~$15.5–17K/unit) implies **~4.7–5.2% — inside the quoted Class A band**.

**Verdict:** $165–222K/unit is a Class B/C + Canyon County + small-deal artifact of a non-disclosure state's thin tape. Institutional 2020s Class A in Ada County: **~$250–340K/unit clearing, ~$300–340K/unit replacement; $328K/unit for a 2024-build at Eagle/I-84 is within market at ≈ replacement parity — not an outlier.** Caution to carry: the sole price-verified new-vintage trade (Telluride, $205/SF) shows smaller/secondary new product can clear near $220K/unit — the $328K valuation leans on institutional scale, 2024 spec, and node quality, supported by the unverified-but-consistent Prelude and Verraso prints.
