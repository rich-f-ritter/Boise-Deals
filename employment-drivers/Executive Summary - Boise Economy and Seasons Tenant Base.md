## I. The Boise Economy — seven need-to-knows

**1. The published job number is wrong-side-of-truth; the tax records say Boise is still growing ~2.4%.** CES payroll growth prints +0.42% T3M YoY (Jun-26) after a four-year staircase down — but QCEW hard counts (UI tax records, ~97% of jobs) show **Ada +2.51% and Canyon +2.09% YoY in Q4-25** vs the CES +0.91% for the same window, with the divergence opening exactly in the shutdown quarter. Sector-level, the hard counts show healthcare **+7.1%**, construction **+6.7%**, hospitality +2.0% — the sectors CES portrays as collapsing — while true softness sits in finance (−1.8%), prof services (−1.5%), manufacturing (−1.4%): the HP/Albertsons white-collar story. The 10-metro cross-check shows the survey undercounted 9 of 10 metros in Q4-25, worst in the fastest-growing (birth-death model fingerprint); Boise re-benchmarks to the tax records in early 2027 — expect upward revision. *[Research paper §1–2; appendices E, J; BLS CES flat file + QCEW API, both re-verified 8/13/26]*

**2. The real slowdown is national and it's a labor-supply event, not a Boise demand failure.** Shift-share: Boise's local outperformance collapsed from +7,869 jobs/yr to +673 — Boise still beats the nation in 7 of 10 sectors; the nation just stopped (calendar-2025 US payrolls: +181K *for the year*; net US migration negative for the first time in ~50 years; break-even payroll growth ≈ zero per Dallas/StL/KC Fed). *[Paper §3; appendix G]*

**3. What's genuinely soft locally is specific and mostly policy/weather:** Idaho's ~$4B of tax cuts → deficit → 4% agency cuts (state gov jobs −7.4% YoY; federal −5.0%); leisure hit by ~50 closures + the worst ski winter in years (Bogus at ~40% of normal snow) — *not* tourism (4th straight record year at BOI); healthcare hiring paused (Blue Cross Medicaid contract loss, small-practice closures) even as hospital job postings run +44%. *[Appendix H; IDOL; BLS BED]*

**4. The labor market is supply-constrained — which is rent-supportive.** Wages 3rd-fastest-growing in the US (+4.7%); SW Idaho postings +11% (semis +92%); unemployment 3.4% vs US 4.4%; no claims spike. The measured labor-force decline (−0.79% T3M — *people*, not jobs) is largely population-control lag plus migrant composition (retirees/remote workers who lease apartments without joining local payrolls). *[Appendices A, H; paper §5]*

**5. Migration re-accelerated to a five-year high — and it's landing on the subject.** Placer device data (through Jun-26, 11 months past Census): net domestic migration **~+11.6–12.4K** YE Jun-26, ~2× the prior year (client confirmed the +8.5K O-D-table sum is not the level); **Boise = #5 of 925 US CBSAs; Ada = #8 of 3,126 US counties**; California = 47% of net inflow; the DC feeder doubled (federal exodus). **ZIP 83642 — the subject's ZIP — is the metro's #1 in-migration ZIP (+2,417, +34.7/1,000, accelerating +1,077 YoY)** while north Meridian decelerated −511. *[Appendix K; data/placerai/]*

**6. Housing math does the demand work payrolls can't:** own-vs-rent spread ~$1,500–1,900/mo (median Meridian house ~$550K at 6–7% vs ~$1,700–1,800 rents); metro pipeline collapsed to ~600 units u/c (−55% YoY; completions −64%); absorption ~2,500+/yr; occupancy 94.3%. *[Appendices D, F; CBRE/Lee/MMG]*

**7. The 2027–2030 catalyst stack is dated and visible; so are the risks.** Catalysts: Micron ID1 first DRAM mid-2027 (+ID2 2028; ~2,000 direct jobs ramping), ~$1.55B hospital openings 2028–30, District at Ten Mile Ph-1 spring-2027 (Target/Life Time/In-N-Out), SH-16 complete 2027, Gowen F-16 2027, $700M airport. Risks: HP's full Boise exit by end-2027 (~1.1–1.7K high-income jobs), Albertsons HQ erosion, Blue Cross selling its Meridian campus (802 jobs), Medicaid cuts landing 2027, Micron memory-cycle risk, Ten Mile supply resuming 2028+ (Southridge 640 u/c is the closest; Vanguard Village 552 entitled = the permit tripwire). *[Paper §7; appendices B, C, H; District dossier]*

**Underwriting posture:** base +1.0–1.5%/yr employment growth; upside +2.0–2.5% if Sept-2026 QCEW confirms; demand underwritten off migration + own-vs-rent + supply trough more than payrolls. **Checkpoints: QCEW Q1-26 (~Sept 2026) · Census Vintage 2026 (Dec-26–Mar-27) · CES benchmark (early 2027).**

## II. The Seasons at Meridian Tenant Base — seven need-to-knows

*(360 units, opened Aug-2024. Rent roll 7/9/26: 342 occupied = 95.0%; demographic file covers 305 units / 613 occupants.)*

**1. Micron is a rounding error here — the diversification thesis is confirmed in the rent roll.** Micron-direct: **9 residents = 2.0% of employed occupants**; Micron-related broad (direct + fab construction + eng/design + semi vendors): **28 = 6.3%**; units with any Micron-related earner: **24 = 7.9% of units**. Contrast Canyon Ridge: 71.5% of employed occupants and ~70% of units Micron-related. CR is a Micron bet; Seasons is a bet on the Meridian economy. *[Comparison wb, Tier Summary/Overview]*

**2. Employer diversification is about as good as it gets:** 366 distinct normalized employers; **largest single employer = St. Luke's at 4.0%** (19 residents); top-5 share 12.4%; top-10 18.5% (CR: 26.4%/38.6%/47.8%). The roster reads like the corridor: St. Luke's 19, Saint Alphonsus 13, Micron 10, Amazon 7, Albertsons 5, Chick-fil-A 5, ICCU 4, West Ada SD 4, State of Idaho 4, ICOM 4. *[Demographic Analysis, Employers; Comparison wb]*

**3. Industry mix mirrors the metro's actual (QCEW-verified) growth engines:** healthcare is #1 at **19.3% of employed** (median $55.2K) — the sector growing +7.1% in Ada hard counts — then construction/trades 13.0% ($58.9K), food service 9.2% ($33.4K), tech/semis 8.4% ($58.5K), professional services 8.4% ($66.0K), government 7.5% ($69.3K). Defensive sectors 26.8% of occupants; cyclical 41.6%. *[Demographic Analysis, Industry Mix]*

**4. This is a young, roommate-heavy, pre-family renter base:** median occupant age **27.5 (city: 37.5)**; **51% of occupants are 18–29**; households with minors only 12.8%; single-occupant 33%, two-person 44% (roommate households 34% of units; couples 22%); avg household size 2.01 vs Meridian ~2.7; guarantor-backed 9.5% of units; 1.24 vehicles/unit; 36% pet penetration. Exactly the profile the own-vs-rent spread traps in rentals — and the churn-prone end of the market. *[Demographic Analysis, Summary/Age/HH Comp; Comparison wb]*

**5. Incomes are solid-workforce, not luxury:** median household income **$80–89K** (two screening bases) — ~40% above the Meridian *renter* median ($61.9K) but below the city all-household median ($100.8K); median *personal* income $58.0K (CR: $107.0K); 50% of households are dual-earner. Distribution: ~35% of households under $75K, ~29% $75–125K, ~23% $100–150K band area, ~13% $150K+. *[Demographic Analysis, HH Income; Comparison wb, Income & Coverage]*

**6. Rent coverage is adequate with a real tail:** median coverage **3.84×** rent (CR: 6.14×); **79% of units ≥3.0×; 8.2% below 2.0× (thin)**; at the median, rent-to-income ~22%; stress table shows +10% rents still leave ~68% under 30% burden. Median contract rent **$1,714 ≈ 11% below the property's own market rents ($1,897)** — embedded loss-to-lease/mark-to-market, and evidence of lease-up concessioning. *[Demographic Analysis, Affordability; Comparison wb, Income & Coverage]*

**7. The current-state flags: 38 units on notice = 11.1% of occupied** (CR: 8.4%), 18 vacant, and the biggest resident cohort (18–24, 26% of occupants, median income $38.4K) is the most churn- and payment-sensitive. Move-ins ran 43–66/quarter through the last four quarters — the 2025 lease cohort is maturing now. Data-quality caveats per the analysis workbook: the seller report's cartesian-join inflation (76 units), 23 salaries keyed monthly (screened basis corrects), ~21% blank employers. *[Demographic Analysis, Data Quality; Comparison wb, Lease-Up & Churn]*

**The synthesis:** the tenant file independently validates the market research. The metro's real growth sectors (healthcare, construction, logistics) are the tenant base's top industries; the corridor's anchor employers appear by name in the roster; the property's ZIP is the metro's #1 migration destination; and Micron — the headline everyone asks about — is 2% of the tenant base directly. The demand engine for this asset is the young, priced-out-of-ownership Meridian workforce, and both the economy research and the rent roll say that engine re-accelerated through mid-2026. The honest watch items are the same on both sides: the 11% notice rate against an 11% mark-to-market gap (churn vs. trade-out math), the thin-coverage tail, and the metro's white-collar erosion touching the top of the renter income distribution.
