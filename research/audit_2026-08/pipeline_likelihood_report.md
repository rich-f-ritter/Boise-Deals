# Proposed Pipeline — Likelihood & Timing
**Seasons at Meridian — 5-mile ring | 16 proposed deals | As of Aug 15, 2026**

**Headline:** 4,494 proposed units in the ring; **1,221 probability-weighted units (27%)** expected to deliver by YE2031. **Exactly one deal (Outer Banks) has a building permit**, and it alone carries 34% of the entire weighted total. Everything else is paper. The binding constraint is not entitlement — it is construction debt: at ~$305–322k/unit and ~55% LTC, new garden supply needs ~$2,203/u/mo against the subject's ~$1,796 (+22%), and ring construction lending has been near zero since 2023. Weight permits, GCs, and closed loans; discount council votes heavily.

**Probability-weighted pipeline: 1,221 units (27.2% of the 4,494 proposed).**

Reading the shape: a single permitted deal is a third of the expected supply, and the two largest paper entitlements in the ring (Vanguard 552u, Record 472u) sit in the bottom half — 1,024 headline units that contribute a combined 161 weighted units. The "Unlikely" tier is where 60% of the headline unit count lives and where 48% of the expectation comes from; that tier is almost entirely deals whose next event is a defensive extension filing rather than a permit.

Two structural observations:
- **Entitlement clocks are the near-term event risk, not hearings.** Four deals (The Judy ~9/10/26, Meridian OZ ~10/17/26, Gateway ~11/19/26, Records already past) face commencement deadlines inside the next 90 days with no extension found. In this market a lapse is not a formality — Outer Banks lapsed and had to re-hear; Victory Flats is on its second extension; 12548 Overland lapsed and died.
- **Product type is doing quiet damage.** Six deals (Record, Cole Denton, Fairview, Pine 43, Heritage Square, Rolling Hill) are podium/structured-parking or vertically-integrated product with a cost basis materially above the $305–322k/unit garden benchmark. Their required rents sit further above $2,203/u/mo than the garden hurdle already is above the subject's $1,796.

| **5-yr total** | **1,221** | ~244 units/yr average |

**Versus the 2022–2024 market (~1,000–3,000 units/yr delivered):**

- **2027–2029 is a genuine supply vacuum.** Three years averaging **120 weighted units/yr** — roughly **4–12%** of the 2022–24 run-rate. That is the window in which the subject's rent and occupancy recovery is structurally protected.
- **2030 is the only year that even approaches the bottom of the historical band** — 735 weighted units, ~74% of the 1,000-unit floor and ~25% of the 3,000-unit ceiling. But 2030 is also the least reliable number on this page: **every unit in it comes from a deal with no building permit today.** If financing does not reopen, 2030 hollows out toward 2031 or disappears; if financing reopens sharply in 2027–28, the 2030 bar could overshoot, because ~2,700 units of entitled-but-dormant optionality (Record, Vanguard, Gateway, Victory Flats, Fairview) would all become financeable at once.
- **Cumulatively, 1,221 units over five years is roughly what this market delivered in a single average year of 2022–2024.** The pipeline is not a supply threat to the subject's hold period; it is a set of call options on a rent level ~22% above today's.
- Practical underwriting read: model 2027–2029 with essentially no new competitive supply beyond Outer Banks' phased lease-up ~3.3 mi west, and treat 2030 as scenario-dependent rather than base-case.
