## Timeline of events

### 2015–2017 — Silence
No kills documented. The subject's own submarket is entitling and building; Meridian's operative comprehensive plan is the pre-2019 document; ACHD's fee schedule still traces to Ordinance 217 (2011) as amended. Boise-MSA garden product costs **$125,874/unit** (2012–16 cohort, n=7). Construction leverage: ~65% LTC as a matter of course.

### 2018 — The first shot: comp-plan supremacy
- **Sept 4, 2018 — Tanner Creek denied (272u).** P&Z had recommended approval; ACHD had signed off; the developer's own traffic case was that his plan cut trips 70% versus the site's prior commercial concept. Council denied anyway. Mayor De Weerd: *"Highly discourage."* Cncl. Milam: *"So many apartments. Apartmented (sic) out."* Cncl. Borton: *"Comp plan amendments are drastic shifts."*
- **The lesson embedded here** — the future-land-use map outranks the traffic engineer — becomes the template for everything that follows.

### 2019 — Politics turns before policy does
- **Nov 2019** — Idaho Statesman frames Treasure Valley municipal elections as *"a referendum on growth"*; incumbents lose seats.
- **Nov 2019/Jan 2020** — Meridian adopts a substantially rewritten Comprehensive Plan, framed at adoption as urbanizing/growth-accommodating. **The document has not been rewritten since.** What changes over the next six years is enforcement intensity, not text.
- **Jul 17, 2019** — Andorra pre-application; staff recommends TN-R. **Oct 10, 2019** — ACHD confirms the 164-unit senior plan generates <1,000 ADT, no TIS required. Nothing about this project is contentious.

### 2020 — Death by paperwork
- **Mar 5, 2020** — Andorra P&Z hearing. Annexation clears in concept.
- **~mid-2020** — **Andorra dies (164u)**: the Development Agreement is never signed, so the annexation ordinance is never adopted. No vote, no opposition, no market event — a self-inflicted process failure, and the closest-in kill to the subject (0.3 mi).
- **2020** — Tanner Creek's second filing (H-2020-0024) is denied again — comp-plan inconsistency plus "lack of commercial development plans."
- **Mar 5, 2020** — Union 93's CUP and 33% height variance clear P&Z; downtown business owners' parking objections are dismissed (Cmsr. McCarvel: *"If you're going to want massive amounts of vehicles, live somewhere else."*). This project will not die politically.

### 2021 — Peak boom, first quiet exits
- Boise MSA effective rent growth **+9.9%**; Meridian submarket **+10.4%** — the cycle top. 10-yr Treasury bottoms near 1.08% in January.
- **Feb 2021** — Galena walks from the Meridian Civic Block public-facility concept to focus on Union 93. *(First of two Civic Block failures.)*
- **~Summer 2021** — WinCo sells the 2600 E Overland grocery pad, next door to the subject.
- **2021** — **Wells St. Assisted Living / Andorra II denied** — "not in the best interest of the City." Second rental-housing failure on the Magic View enclave in two years.
- **Sept 17, 2021** — **Madison Town Square withdraws (251u)**, three days before its first Boise P&Z hearing, after staff urged design alternatives. No hearing, no findings, no public testimony ever occurs. It will sit dormant 33 months before anyone calls it dead.

### 2022 — The hinge year: 1,095 units die
- **Mar–Nov 2022** — Fed funds go 0–0.25% → ~4.00%; 10-yr Treasury 1.76% → mid-3s. ACHD Ordinance 246A takes effect Oct 1 (MF low-rise fee $1,895/unit).
- **May 17, 2022** — BoiseDev reports Overland & Wells II: 351 units on the WinCo pad **touching the subject**.
- **Jun 13, 2022** — Cobalt Point announced (264u, 0.85 mi). **Jul 26** — CUP filed. **Aug 8** — a 20-page ACHD comment lands.
- **Aug 4, 2022** — **Meridian P&Z votes 3-1 to recommend denial of Overland & Wells II, against its own staff's approval recommendation.**
- **Sept 15 / Oct 20, 2022** — Cobalt Point continued twice; by Oct 24 the city's own noticing file is labeled *"Applicant request withdrawl."*
- **Nov 9, 2022** — Cobalt Point formally withdraws (264u). **Same week**, P&Z recommends denial of The Bridge (549u) on height/parking.
- **Sept 2022** — **Union 93's contractors walk off unpaid.** The equity partner is gone. Two half-built podiums will stand fenced across from City Hall for four years.
- **Oct 25 / Nov 9, 2022** — **Overland & Wells II DENIED; Findings of Denial adopted.** Three grounds stacked in one document: employment-land loss, failing Overland/Eagle turning movements, and **MF over-concentration adjacent to the subject's own 360-unit Phase 1**. An MDA denial is not subject to judicial review (*Brown v. City of Meridian*). This is the doctrinal template.
- **Nov 16, 2022** — Ada County BOCC denies Lake Hazel & Five Mile 3-0 (130u); ~20 residents testify against, one for. Cmsr. Davidson: *"this was zoned commercial… it seems more appropriate to keep it."*
- **Nov 28, 2022** — Idaho Statesman: *"Hamstrung City Council approves apartment development despite congestion concerns"* — Meridian's council is publicly straining against its own approval obligations weeks before it starts denying.
- **Full-year 2022** — Meridian permits **1,048 MF units** (a cycle peak); Boise MSA finances **3,043 units / $554.9M** of construction loans, **5 of them inside the 5-mile ring.**

### 2023 — Denial gives way to attrition: 999 units die without a single "no"
- **Jan 3, 2023** — Council **reverses** its own P&Z and approves The Bridge's 549 units and 85-ft height. Cncl. Pres. Hoaglun: *"height and what the plan is for this location fits well."* The entitlement is not the problem.
- **Q1 2023** — Boise MSA plans just **596 MF units in the first four months**, an ~87% collapse from the 2022 pace. July 2023 Boise MF permits run **80% below** July 2022.
- **Mar 2023** — **Meridian OZ Apartments denied (60u)** — the year's only true denial, driven by a single 45-year resident's testimony and a procedural ACHD-drainage objection. Mayor Simison: *"Best of luck in working with the owners and neighbors."*
- **Jun 15, 2023** — **Fairview Apartments withdrawn (150u).** Agenda item 6, verbatim: *"Applicant Requests a Withdrawal."* No findings, no fight.
- **~Late 2023** — **The 10 at Meridian's CUP expires (559u).** The largest single kill in the register, and the one with the least drama: three hearings, one mild written objection across 3.5 years, unanimous or near-unanimous approvals, and then nothing was built. P&Z Chair Seal, 2021: *"we have nobody here to dispute that, which is probably the first time ever I have heard of that in Meridian."*
- **Oct 31, 2023** — **Civic Block withdrawn (230u).** River Caddis's stated arithmetic: valuations down ~$175,000/unit since 2021, costs up ~$130,000/unit. Second developer to abandon the same city-owned block.
- **Aug 2023** — A Boise-area developer reports sending financing requests to **50 lenders, getting interest from 3**, at 7.5–10.5% versus 3.5–4.25% in 2021.

### 1. Capital markets / cost — 2,362 units (54%)
*The Bridge 549, The 10 559, Union 93 350, Civic Block 230, Newkirk 216, Hummingbird 170, Fairview 150, Artisan 138.*

Six of these eight had **approvals in hand** and died anyway. The cause is not contested — it is on the record in the developers' own words: CenterCal ("the market… has caught up"; "construction costs, lending costs"), Conger ("we have yet to find a partner"; "economically unviable"), Robnett ("rates spiked, numbers stopped making sense"), River Caddis (valuations −$175k/unit, costs +$130k/unit), Galena's counsel (the back-end equity partner "backed out in 2022").

**Underwriting implication:** entitlement is not the binding constraint in this submarket and has not been since 2022. Any supply forecast built off approved-unit counts will systematically overstate deliveries. The operative doctrine — *an approved project with no construction loan and no permit is not supply* — is validated at the 2,362-unit level by this register alone. Corollary: **discount the pipeline by capital-stack status, not by entitlement status.** Of the ~2,565 in-ring units carrying 2027–2029 delivery estimates, at least one 552-unit line (Vanguard Village) is a CoStar UC flag with no city permit found.

### 2. Land-use policy — 871 units (20%)
*Overland & Wells II 351, Tanner Creek 272, Lake Hazel & Five Mile 248 (two attempts on one pad), Magic View II (units unknown). Plus Avani's 35 zoned-away acres.*

A coherent, repeatable doctrine, first fully articulated in the Nov 9, 2022 Overland & Wells II findings and reused since: **(a)** conversion of employment/commercial-designated land to residential is a net loss to the city; **(b)** failing intersection LOS is an independent ground; **(c)** multifamily over-concentration adjacent to existing apartment phases is a third; and **(d)** procedurally, an MDA applicant must show the change is *superior to the existing Development Agreement* — a burden the applicant, not the city, carries, and which is not subject to judicial review.

Ada County codified the same instinct: MF barred outright in commercial zones (Sept 2025), and Condition #15 on the Hazelton plat is an affirmative contractual bar, not a lapsed entitlement.

**Underwriting implication:** this cause is *asymmetrically protective of the subject.* The doctrine's strongest application in the entire register was applied to the parcel physically touching Seasons at Meridian, explicitly to protect against over-concentration next to its own Phase 1. But note the counter-current: Avani (2024) and Rolling Hill (2026) both show Council overriding staff/P&Z in the *pro-development* direction when it suits — the doctrine is discretionary, not mechanical.

### 3. Entitlement lapse / self-inflicted process failure — 415 units (10%)
*Andorra 164, Madison Town Square 251.*

Andorra's Development Agreement was simply never signed — and in Meridian a signed DA is a legal precondition to adopting the annexation ordinance, so an approval-in-principle evaporates. Madison pulled its own applications three days before the hearing over unresolved design objections whose substance was never made public (BoiseDev's public-records request contents were never reported).

**Underwriting implication:** entitlement-stage attrition is invisible in any pipeline feed. Neither project ever produced a vote, a finding, or a news event at the time of death. Where a competitor's entitlement is old and unbuilt, **verify the DA/ordinance actually executed**, not just that a body voted yes.

### 4. Site / infrastructure — 418 units (10%)
*Cobalt Point 264, Rolling Hill 154.*

Rolling Hill's cause is unusually clean: one landowner mid-footprint wouldn't sell, leaving an un-annexed Ada County enclave inside the boundary and an undeliverable roadway. Shrinking the footprint to exclude the holdout in 2025 made the roadway *more* undeliverable, producing a second denial recommendation. Cobalt Point's is inferred: two continuances after a 20-page ACHD comment, a last-minute redesign, then withdrawal.

**Underwriting implication:** infill sites in built commercial parks and mixed-ownership corridors carry an assembly/access risk that greenfield does not. This is now compounded structurally — ACHD Ordinance 254 deleted multifamily's published impact-fee number effective 3/1/2026, so a competitor can no longer price its own traffic fee from a table.

### 5. Affordable-finance mechanics — 239 units (5%)
*Centrepoint.* Approved by Council, then killed by the loss of a HUD Difficult-to-Develop-Area basis boost, Idaho's first-ever competitive 4% tax-exempt bond round, and a seller demanding an immediate post-hearing close.

**Underwriting implication:** affordable is now the *only* structure closing construction loans in this market (5% of financed units 2020–22 → **23% in 2023–26**; the only 2026 origination in the whole Boise-MSA tape is IHFA-supported), which means affordable-supply risk is now governed by allocation politics, not by market rents. It is a poor proxy for market-rate supply risk in either direction. Separately: **no LIHTC award in Ada County 2024–2026 falls inside the 5-mile ring** — all eleven cluster in Boise's core, Bench, and foothills, 5.9–10 miles away.

### 6. Neighborhood opposition as decisive — 60 units (1%)
*Meridian OZ Apartments.* One project, sixty units, one testifying neighbor.

**Underwriting implication — this is the register's most counterintuitive finding.** Organized opposition is *not* what kills multifamily here. The 549-unit Bridge had its nearest single-family homes 1,700 feet away and no public opposition. The 559-unit "The 10" drew a P&Z chair's remark that it was the first Meridian project he'd seen with zero opposition. Gateway at Ten Mile drew 80+ opposition letters and was approved **unanimously**. Rolling Hill's most vocal residents testified *in favor* because they wanted to sell. Where opposition did prove decisive (Lake Hazel & Five Mile), it worked by aligning with a comprehensive-plan argument the county already believed. **Do not underwrite NIMBY risk as the primary supply brake — underwrite capital.**

## Eras

### Era I — Permissive boom (2015 – mid-2019)
**What killed deals:** essentially nothing. One denial in five years (Tanner Creek, 2018), on comp-plan grounds, over the objections of the city's own P&Z *and* ACHD. Costs $125,874/unit (2012–16) rising to $163,805/unit (2017–19). 65% LTC construction leverage is standard. The 10-yr Treasury averages 2.32%.
**Failure mode:** singular and political — a Council willing, once, to enforce its future-land-use map against a project everyone else had blessed. Not a pattern yet.
⚠ Coverage caveat: 2015–17 was never primary-searched. The absence of kills here is partly an absence of research.

### Era II — Political turn, process attrition (mid-2019 – 2021)
**What killed deals:** paperwork and pre-hearing staff friction, not votes. Andorra dies because a DA is never signed. Madison withdraws three days before its hearing over design comments. Magic View II is denied on a bare "not in the best interest of the City" annexation finding. Meanwhile the market is at its absolute top: Boise MSA rent growth **+9.9%**, Meridian **+10.4%** (2021); Boise MSA posts the **highest in-migration rate in the nation (+67%)**.
**Failure mode change:** from *contested denial* to *quiet self-termination*. Three of the era's four kills produced no public testimony and no findings document. The electoral signal (2019 "referendum on growth") arrives well before any hearing-record doctrine does.

### Era III — Rate shock and doctrinal hardening (2022 – 2023)
**What killed deals:** everything at once. Fed funds 0→5.50% in 16 months; 10-yr peaks at ~4.80% (Oct 2023). **2,094 units die across these two years — 48% of the entire twelve-year total.** The composition inverts mid-era:
- *2022 is the denial year.* Overland & Wells II establishes the three-part doctrine (employment land + failing intersections + MF over-concentration) and Ada County denies Lake Hazel on comp-plan grounds. 1,095 units.
- *2023 is the withdrawal year.* Not a single large project is denied. Fairview withdraws, Civic Block withdraws, The 10's CUP lapses, Meridian OZ (60u) is the only actual denial. 999 units, essentially all self-terminated. Boise MSA MF planning collapses ~87% year-over-year; **zero construction loans close in the 5-mile ring.**

**Failure mode change:** politics and capital swap places mid-era. From 2023 onward, the city rarely has to say no — the capital stack says it first. Note the pivot point precisely: The Bridge is *denied* by P&Z in Nov 2022 on height and parking, *approved* by Council in Jan 2023, and *abandoned by its own developer* in Aug 2024. Same project, three regimes.

### Era IV — Demand-side capitulation and conversion (2024 – 2026)
**What killed deals:** the developers themselves, on the record, citing demand and cost. The Bridge (549u, approved, walked), Newkirk (216u, approved, converted), Hummingbird (170u, approved, lapsed), Centrepoint (239u, approved, financing collapsed), Artisan (138u, platted, sponsor exiting). **1,584 units in three years, of which 1,312 had approvals in hand at time of death.**

The regulatory environment simultaneously *loosens* in places (Boise's zoning rewrite effective Dec 2023 produced ~2,000 infill homes in year two; Council overrode P&Z to approve Rolling Hill 200u in May 2026 and Gateway 390u unanimously in Nov 2024) and *hardens* in others (Ada County bans MF in commercial zones Sept 2025; Condition #15 at Hazelton; ACHD's Ordinance 254 deletes MF's published impact fee).

**Failure mode change:** the register stops being about entitlement at all. Land converts *away* from rental — to retail ground leases (2600 E Overland, Colliers, since Jan 2024), to for-sale homes (Newkirk, Avani, Latitude Forty Three), to an RV dealership (1005 E Fairview), to a commercial plat with an MF prohibition (Hazelton), to an aquarium (60 N Cole). Meridian MF permits: 1,048 (2022) → **175 (2024)** → **164 (2025)**. Deliveries in the Meridian submarket in 2025: **zero.**

# Executive Summary: Why Development Is Harder Today Than It Was

1. **All-in cost roughly doubled, then stopped — the problem is no longer cost inflation, it's the level.** Boise-MSA garden product went $125,874/unit (2012–16) → $163,805 (2017–19) → $291,375 (2022 vintage) — ~9%/yr compounding — and then **plateaued at ~+1.7%/yr**, landing at **$304,530–$322,530/unit** today (Quarterra's June 2026 Emblem Meridian filing at $304,530/unit / $324 psf; ~$322,530/unit to replicate the subject's resort-amenity spec). National indices kept running ~4%/yr (Turner Q3 2025 +4.19% YoY) — local costs decoupled *downward* because almost nobody is bidding.

2. **Leverage fell 1,000 bps and the equity check jumped 38%.** Construction LTC went from ~65% (through 2022) to **55%** (2024+), taking required equity from **$99.6k to $137.0k per unit**. That is the single cleanest explanation for why approvals stopped converting into starts: the same building now needs materially more equity at a moment when the exit is worth less.

3. **New supply needs ~$2,203/unit/month; the subject collects ~$1,796 — a 22% gap.** That figure is *after* normalizing Emblem Meridian's proforma ancillary income, which was inflated **+83% versus the subject's actuals**. This is the arithmetic behind every developer quote in the graveyard: CenterCal's *"now it's caught up,"* Conger's *"economically unviable,"* Robnett's *"numbers stopped making sense."*

4. **Construction lending in the 5-mile ring is functionally closed and has been for three and a half years.** Volume is **−82% from the 2022 peak** (3,043 units financed → 560 in 2025). In-ring originations: 5 loans (2022) → **0 (2023)** → 1 (2024) → 2 (2025) → **0 YTD 2026** — **three loans total since Jan 2023, one of them income-restricted.** Affordable's share of financed units went from 5% (2020–22) to **23% (2023–26)**, and the only 2026 origination in the entire Boise-MSA tape is IHFA-supported.

5. **Meridian's permit series confirms it from the city's own reports: 1,048 MF units permitted in 2022 → 175 in 2024 → 164 in 2025.** An 84% collapse, with 2025 the low. 164 units citywide for a year is roughly one mid-size project. The Meridian submarket recorded **zero net completions in 2025** (CoStar, per the Quarterra equity book).

6. **Union 93 is the canonical cost-overrun failure, and it took six years to resolve.** A fully entitled 350-unit downtown project (CUP + 33% height variance, March 2020) broke ground, got ~40–50% built, then lost its Opportunity-Zone back-end equity partner in 2022. Contractors walked in September 2022 unpaid; $22M in mechanic's-lien judgments by September 2024; Ada County Sheriff's foreclosure auction October 2025. Ahlquist + The Pacific Companies relaunched it in July 2026 as Heritage Square — **250 units, 100 fewer than the original, breaking ground Q1 2027 for 2029 completion.**

7. **Entitlement politics hardened into an articulable, reusable doctrine — and it was applied to the parcel touching this subject.** The Nov 9, 2022 findings denying Overland & Wells II (351u) cited employment-land loss, failing Overland/Eagle turning movements, and **MF over-concentration next to Seasons' own 360-unit Phase 1** — plus the procedural hook that an MDA applicant must show the change is *superior to the existing Development Agreement*. Meridian's own record notes MDA denials are not subject to judicial review (*Brown v. City of Meridian*).

8. **Traffic and infrastructure moved from an argument to a toll booth.** ACHD's single-family impact fee rose **$3,493 → $5,803/unit (+66%) in 3.5 years** (Ord. 246A eff. 10/1/2022 → Ord. 254 eff. 3/1/2026), driven by VMT cost +42% and a switch to ITE 12th Edition. More consequential: **Ordinance 254 deleted multifamily's table fee entirely** — every new MF project must now go through individual assessment with no published number. And ITD coordination can freeze a file indefinitely: Latitude Forty Three (0.3 mi) got a 6-0 P&Z recommendation on April 3, 2025 and has been continued repeatedly since — most recently April 7, 2026 — pending an ITD traffic impact analysis.

9. **Developers now downsize on purpose, and the city notices.** Meridian's own deputy community development director, Caleb Hood, on Newkirk's 216 apartments becoming 95 for-sale homes: *"More typical is development attempting to get more density per acre, not less."* Every revived site in this register came back smaller or later: The 10 559→516; Union 93 350→250; Centrepoint 239→213; Meridian OZ 60→36. Only Rolling Hill grew (154→200) — and only after a two-year fight and a second staff denial recommendation.

10. **The one thing that is *not* harder: winning a vote.** Organized opposition accounts for exactly **60 of 4,365 killed units (1.4%)**. Gateway at Ten Mile drew 80+ opposition letters and passed unanimously (Nov 2024). Council overrode its own P&Z to approve The Bridge (Jan 2023), Avani (Mar 2024), and Rolling Hill 5-1 (May 2026). ⚠ Honest counterweight: this generalization does not hold in **unincorporated Ada County**, where organized opposition (Southwest Ada County Alliance) killed the same 11.4-acre pad twice and produced a permanent MF prohibition.

11. **Land is converting away from rental faster than it is converting toward it.** Since 2023 in this ring: 18 acres at 2600 E Overland re-marketed as retail ground leases (Colliers, since Jan 2024) and lot-split by WinCo Feb 2025; 1005 E Fairview re-tenanted as an RV dealership (Apr 2024); Newkirk's 216 units → 95 for-sale homes; Avani's 35 comp-plan-designated MF/TH acres → 256 detached homes; the Hazelton pad → 12 commercial lots with MF contractually prohibited; 60 N Cole → an aquarium expansion; the Magic View enclave → a 79-lot for-sale plat after three failed rental attempts.

12. **Where evidence is thin, say so.** 2015–2017 was never primary-searched — no sweep in this effort achieved access to Meridian, Boise, or Ada County hearing archives for that window. Three unit counts are unrecoverable (Magic View II, Winters Run, the 1999 Franklin DA). **Foxcroft's 216 apartments (Ten Mile & Pine, ~3.8 mi) have an entirely unverified status since March 2021** — they are either live pipeline or a fifth-largest graveyard entry, and we do not know which. The Artisan Victory Market entry (138u, ~0.85 mi) rests on a single snippet-level source. Cobalt Point's and Fairview's causes are *inferred from timing and behavior*, not from any stated rationale — neither ever produced a finding, a vote, or a readable withdrawal letter.

## So what — for underwriting a stabilized asset here

**1. The barrier to new supply is a quantified moat, not a narrative one.** A competitor must clear **~$2,203/unit/month** to justify ~$305–322k/unit at 55% LTC. The subject collects **~$1,796** — a **22% gap**, or roughly **$407/unit/month × 360 units ≈ $1.76M/yr of NOI** that a new entrant must find and the subject does not. At the deal's cap rate that gap is worth roughly $35M of value that new supply cannot access. That is the moat, and it is not a forecast — it is arithmetic off a competitor's own filed proforma.

**2. Underwrite the pipeline by capital stack, not by entitlement.** Of the 4,365 killed units, **1,312 units across six projects held approvals at the moment they died**. Three construction loans have closed in the entire 5-mile ring since January 2023 — one income-restricted. Applied to today's roster: strip any line without a confirmed permit *and* a confirmed loan. Doing so removes at least Vanguard Village's 552 units (CoStar UC flag, no city permit found) from the ~2,565-unit 2027–2029 forecast, taking it to ~2,013.

**3. Time is on the asset's side for at least 24 months, structurally.** Meridian recorded **zero net deliveries in 2025**. Every in-ring project carrying a delivery estimate targets **2027–2029** — none targets 2026. Even the most credible new entrant, Quarterra's Emblem Meridian (250u, 0.98 mi), filed only in June 2026 for **2028** delivery. Tanner Creek's 280 re-entitled units are contractually barred from occupancy until the Linder Rd. overpass finishes (Phase 2 under construction to late 2027) — realistic delivery 2028+. The nearest large approved competitor, Rolling Hill (200u, 0.95 mi, approved May 2026), had no permits or groundbreaking as of August 2026.

**4. The subject's immediate node carries an unusually protective precedent.** The parcel physically touching Seasons at Meridian was denied 351 units in October 2022 on grounds that expressly included over-concentration next to the subject's own Phase 1 — a finding that cuts the same way for any future applicant on that node. WinCo has owned it throughout, has marketed it as retail ground leases since January 2024, formally lot-split it in February 2025, and has filed nothing residential in four years. Revival probability ~5%, earliest ~2030.

**5. Concessions have already burned off, which means rent growth here is currently *effective*, not headline.** Across the subject's own comp set, concessions fell from ~13–14% of asking rent (Nov 2025) to **~2% (Apr 2026)** while asking rent rose $2.08 → $2.20 psf (+5.8%). Comp occupancy held 93.0–94.8%. The submarket occupancy trough was **82.4% in 2024** — a full year later and 440 bps deeper than the MSA's 86.8% trough in 2023 — and recovered above 90% in 2025 on zero deliveries.

**6. But price the volatility: Meridian amplifies the cycle in both directions.** Peak-to-trough rent-growth swing was **14.8 points for Meridian (+10.4% → −4.4%)** versus 12.7 for the MSA. And CoStar's own forward curve does **not** show a straight recovery — Meridian occupancy dips again to **88.5% in 2027F** before resuming, because the submarket carries **8.2% of existing stock under construction** versus 5.1% MSA-wide. The moat is real; the glide path is not smooth.

### Counter-argument: what would make supply reappear fast

Be honest that this moat is a **financing moat, not a zoning moat** — and financing moats close faster than zoning moats.

- **Rate relief is the whole trade.** SOFR is 3.62% and the 10-yr is 4.68% — the Fed has already cut ~175–200bps off the 2023 peak. Another 100–150bps of construction-debt cost, or a return to 65% LTC, mathematically reverses the equity/unit move (−38% back toward $99.6k) and drops the required rent well below $2,203. Nothing else has to change.
- **The entitled inventory is enormous and sitting there.** Outer Banks 516u (CUP refreshed 2024, developer targeting a 2026 groundbreak), Gateway at Ten Mile 390u (approved unanimously), The District at Ten Mile ~1,800 residential units (retail already under construction since May 2026), Pine 43's up-to-604 vertically-integrated shadow units, Records 472u (dormant CUP), Rolling Hill 200u, Heritage Square 250u, Graycliff's 224-unit R-40 block, Tanner Creek 280u. **This is not a market that would need to re-entitle — it would need only to finance.** The 2021 precedent is instructive: The 10 went from filing to full 516-unit entitlement in *eight months* with zero opposition.
- **Political risk to market-rate MF is demonstrably low right now.** Council overrode its own P&Z to approve The Bridge (2023), Avani (2024), Gateway despite 80+ opposition letters (2024), and Rolling Hill 5-1 (2026). Strader's *"ready for its next phase"* is the operative posture on the subject's own Overland corridor.
- **Costs are flat, not rising** (~+1.7%/yr since 2022), so waiting no longer costs a developer much — which cuts both ways: it removes the urgency to build *and* removes the penalty for restarting.
- **Merchant builders are explicitly underwriting into this trough.** Quarterra filed Emblem at 55% LTC for 2028 delivery citing a Meridian pipeline "down nearly 50% from 2023" and zero 2025 deliveries. Sophisticated capital is already positioning for the reversal — which means the subject's competitive window is being underwritten by others as *closing*, not open-ended.
- **Demand fundamentals never broke.** Meridian added **+4,730 residents in the most recent year** (to 152,070, now Idaho's second-largest city, +29.3% since 2020); Ada+Canyon is at 876,760 (+20.8% in six years); Idaho ranked #2 nationally for growth in 2025; Micron's $15B+ expansion and two fabs are under construction; Ada-area employment grew 7% over three years versus 5.9% population. Rent-to-income sits at **21–26%** against a 30% threshold. The demand that would make new supply pencil at $2,203 is not far away — it is a rent-growth cycle away.

**Net:** underwrite the moat as worth roughly **2–3 years of protected rent growth**, not a hold-period-long structural advantage. The correct exit-risk question is not "will Meridian approve apartments?" (it will) but "what does the delivery schedule look like in the 24 months *after* construction debt reopens, given ~2,000–2,600 already-entitled in-ring units queued behind it?"
