## D. The 2022-24 delivery wave and absorption

**THEN (pre-wave):** Boise MSA net completions ran ~900-2,400 units/yr from 2016-2021 (steadily increasing but roughly matched by absorption, keeping occupancy in the 92-95% band). Meridian submarket ran a similar, smaller-scale pattern (roughly 200-1,000 units/yr 2016-2019) before a first mini-wave in 2020 pulled its occupancy down to 88.5%.

**NOW (the wave and its aftermath):**
- Boise MSA: **"peak supply in 2023 and 2024 with over 3,000 units delivered annually"** (direct quote, equity book) against a **36,478-unit total base** — i.e., the metro added roughly **8-9% of its entire existing stock in two years**. Absorption undershot completions in 2022-2023 (driving the occupancy crash) before catching up and then exceeding supply from 2025 onward.
- Meridian submarket: "heavy supply cycle from 2018-2024" on a **10,823-unit base**, with **zero net completions in 2025** allowing absorption to run ahead of supply and pull occupancy back above 90%.
- **Corroborating, independent evidence from this repo's own construction-loan tape** (Yardi export, 118 loans/84 properties, Boise MSA 2012-2026 — `research/audit_2026-08/construction_loan_analysis.json`): financed-unit volume **peaked at 3,043 units / $554.9M in 2022**, then fell to **1,098 (2023) → 1,025 (2024) → 560 units / $70.0M (2025)** — an **82% unit decline / 87% dollar decline from the 2022 peak**. Inside the subject's 5-mile ring specifically: 5 loans in 2022 → **zero in 2023** → 1 (125 units) in 2024 → 2 (368 units) in 2025 → zero in 2026 YTD. This independently confirms the "construction lending in the 5-mile ring has been near zero since 2023" premise given in the assignment brief.

**Magnitude:** The 2022-24 wave delivered on the order of **~9,600+ units metro-wide** (3 years × ~3,000+/yr, order-of-magnitude — exact annual unit counts are chart-read estimates, not data-labeled, so treat as approximate) against a ~33,000-36,000-unit base, i.e., a **~27-29% stock increase in three years** — a genuinely large supply shock, which is why occupancy cratered before the pipeline itself cratered even harder in response (financed volume down 82-87%).

## 7. Merchant builders underwriting 2028+ deliveries to catch the supply trough

**Claim:** Merchant developers are deliberately timing new starts for 2027-2029 delivery, betting that today's ~55% LTC / thin-margin environment clears by then and they land into a supply-scarce, higher-rent market.

**THEN (2015-2021):** No forward-dated delivery discipline is evident pre-2022 — the subject's own 2022 build (360u, $102.5M, 65% LTC) was underwritten and delivered inside a normal ~18-24 month cycle with no multi-year timing bet.

**NOW (2024-2026):** The clearest, best-documented example is:
- **Emblem Meridian (Quarterra)** — 250 units (apartments + townhomes), ~13 ac at N Eagle & Victory (0.98 mi — the nearest live MF filing to the subject). Formal early plans filed **June 2026**; underwritten by Quarterra at **55.0% LTC** ($42.88M loan / $77.96M total cost = $304,530/unit) targeting **2028 delivery**, explicitly because Quarterra's own equity book states the Meridian pipeline is "down nearly 50% from 2023" with **zero Meridian deliveries in 2025** — i.e., Quarterra is underwriting directly into the scarcity window. CORROBORATED this session by two live headlines: **"Mix of townhomes, apartments planned near growing Meridian corner"** (BoiseDev, Jun 11, 2026) and **"Apartments eyed along busy Eagle Road in Meridian. What early plans show"** (Idaho Statesman, Jun 21, 2026) — both match the site, date, and product mix in the audit record; full text not accessible this session, but headline/date/outlet triangulation is strong. Deep underwriting detail: `research/audit_2026-08/replacement_cost_analysis.md`.

Beyond Emblem, the pattern is **systemic across the whole proposed pipeline**, not a one-off — of the roster's PROPOSED/UNDER-CONSTRUCTION rows carrying an estimated-delivery date, essentially none target 2026, and the cluster is 2027-2029:

| Project | Units | Distance | Est. delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Ridge II | 164 | 2.98 mi | Q2 2027 |
| Dorado Station | 212 | 4.91 mi | Q3 2027 |
| Summertown (remaining) | 72 | 3.47 mi | Q3 2027 |
| Outer Banks | 516 | 4.10 mi | Q4 2027 |
| Centrepoint | 213 | 2.85 mi | Q1 2028 |
| Vanguard Village | 552 | 3.48 mi | Q1 2028 (see caveat) |
| The Judy (Maple Grove & Overland) | 162 | 3.44 mi | Q1 2028 |
| Rolling Hill | 200 | 1.00 mi | Q1 2029 |

| **Total** | **2,565** | | **all 2027-2029** |

Source for the table: `research/audit_2026-08/supply_map_data.json` (this repo's own audited/geocoded roster).

**Important caveat on this table (do not take it at face value):** **Vanguard Village** (552u, the single largest line) is explicitly flagged in the audit as **"CoStar carries UC/Q1 2028 but no city permit found — verify."** Per this analysis's own stated underwriting rule, *"an approved project with no construction loan and no permit is NOT supply"* — so this 552-unit line may be paper pipeline (a CoStar data artifact), not a real merchant-builder commitment. Strip it out and the verified 2027-2029 total is **2,013 units across 9 projects**, still a clear multi-year forward-load, but meaningfully smaller and more defensible.

**Magnitude:** Roughly $2,000-2,565 in-ring units are on record targeting 2027-2029 delivery and essentially zero for 2026 — a hard confirmation, independent of the Emblem case study, that the pipeline as a whole is timed to the trough, not to today's rents.
