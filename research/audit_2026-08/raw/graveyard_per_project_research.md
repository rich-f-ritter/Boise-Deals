No evidence of organized neighborhood opposition was found. The site is **under active construction** (started ~Nov 2025) on a scaled-back, all-retail Phase II — evolved from the April 2025 filing (65,000 sf / 3 single-story buildings) to a current ~90,000-100,000 sf / ~6-building, 12-retail-plus-4-restaurant program that is roughly **90% pre-leased** to national names (Apple, Capital Grille, Pottery Barn, Kendra Scott, Vuori, Alo, Solidcore, etc.), opening in phases from fall 2026 through Q1 2027. **Revival risk for multifamily here is effectively nil** for the foreseeable future: CenterCal is a retail/mixed-use operator, not a housing developer, it walked from an already-approved MF entitlement once already, and the parcel is now mid-construction as income-producing retail with 90% of GLA already leased — there is no scenario in which this reverts to apartments before the current retail phase stabilizes and likely not for a full hold period beyond that.

**IC narrative:**
The Bridge is the cleanest demand-side supply kill in the 5-mile ring: a fully entitled, 549-unit, six-story apartment tower at The Village at Meridian — approved by City Council in January 2023 over its own Planning Commission's objection — was voluntarily abandoned by the developer 19 months later, with CenterCal's own VP stating on the record that "the market was underserved for multi-family housing… now it's caught up" and that construction and lending costs no longer supported the residential component. **IC narrative:**

This is now a legally closed door, not a dormant entitlement: revival risk is effectively zero without a fresh DA fight the county has already shown twice it will not grant. For underwriting the Seasons at Meridian ring, this reads as one more ~4-mile-out MF kill driven by comp-plan/employment-and-commercial-land protection rather than by the traffic or capital-markets constraints dominating other dead deals in the register.

**IC narrative:** The original 272-unit Tanner Creek apartment plan died twice (2018, 2020) not on traffic or capacity grounds — P&Z and ACHD both supported it — but because Meridian's Council read it as a Future Land Use Map violation with no offsetting commercial/employment land, producing blunt council-floor rhetoric ("apartmented out," "highly discourage") even against a project that reduced trip generation 70% versus the prior commercial concept. Net read for the subject: this is Meridian's clearest documented template for "MF gets approved here only alongside a comp-plan fix plus real commercial/employment land plus an infrastructure trigger" — a bar high enough, and a timeline long enough (Linder overpass + capital-markets drought), that Tanner Creek is not a near-term supply threat, but it is a live, re-armed 280-unit pipeline item worth flagging on the watch list rather than treating as closed.

### 2. Avani Subdivision — Ten Mile Interchange comp-plan apartment/townhome designation overridden for 256 single-family homes

This is not a denied apartment *project* — it is a Council decision that **killed the multifamily zoning intent of a specific parcel** inside the same Ten Mile corridor the Statesman piece covers, via an unusual staff-and-P&Z-recommended-denial-then-Council-override sequence. Flagging it because it is a documented, quote-rich example of the corridor's supply ceiling being *narrowed* by policy choice rather than market failure.

**Snapshot:** Conger Group (rep. attorney Hethe Clark) / DevCo Residential Group named as co-developer in earlier coverage | 256 single-family homes (0 apartments/townhomes — the site's 2007 Comp Plan designation called for a mixed apartment/townhome/commercial "walkable" district) | 35 acres SE of Franklin Rd & Black Cat Rd, Meridian ID, Ten Mile Interchange Specific Area (~4.0 mi W of subject, approx.; precise lat/lng UNVERIFIED) | File number UNVERIFIED | First proposed: UNVERIFIED (pre-Feb 2024) | P&Z recommended denial **Feb 1, 2024**; Council **approved** 3-2 (i.e., the MF/TH designation died, not the project) reported **March 15, 2024** | Months in process from application to vote: UNVERIFIED, appears short (P&Z-to-Council within ~6 weeks)

**Timeline:**
- **2007** — City adopts the **Ten Mile Interchange Specific Area Plan**, targeting the Star-to-Linder corridor straddling I-84 for "mostly apartments, town houses and commercial" at a minimum density (staff cited 8-15 du/ac, target ~12) to create a walkable mixed-use district.
- **Pre-Feb 2024** — Conger Group/DevCo files Avani Subdivision: 256 detached SF homes at 7.59 du/ac, no townhome component, on the 35-ac SE Franklin/Black Cat corner.
- **Feb 1, 2024 — P&Z Commission** recommends **denial**, agreeing with staff that density and housing mix were "inconsistent with the target density desired" and lacked required townhomes.
- **~March 12, 2024 — City Council** hearing (reported March 15, 2024). Council voted **3-2 to approve**, reversing both staff and P&Z:
  - *For:* Council members **Doug Taylor**, **Luke Cavener**, **Little Roberts**. Taylor: *"I don't think it's appropriate that an outdated comprehensive plan dictates decisions made today when the conditions on the ground are so different from the vision we envisioned years ago."* Cavener, on hearing from residents: *"[There's] too much multifamily development in Meridian... This is kind of unique in that a single-family housing complex... is different than what we're seeing in that area."*
  - *Against:* Council President **Joe Borton** and Vice President **Liz Strader**. Borton: *"When staff, who are subject-matter experts in this, who rarely make [a denial] recommendation — it gives me pause... Is it the right time? Is this the right project? I think what's described is an attractive application that isn't ready for this location at this time."* Strader warned that "granting exceptions risks eroding planning standards" and said Avani "looks very similar to subdivisions all across the city" — the opposite of the 2007 plan's intent.
  - Councilmember **John Overton** was absent.
- Approval was conditioned on finalizing terms at the **April 2, 2024** council meeting, with Council retaining authority to rescind if terms proved unsatisfactory.
- **Post-approval status (as of this session):** UNVERIFIED — no confirmation found of groundbreaking, platting, or construction start as of Aug 2026; treat as approved-but-status-unconfirmed, not as built supply and not as a live MF threat (it's now zoned SF).

**Why it died (as multifamily potential):** **Land-use policy override, in reverse of the usual pattern seen elsewhere in this ring.** Here the city's own 17-year-old comp plan *wanted* apartments/townhomes on this land; Council chose to let a for-sale developer build zero of them, explicitly reasoning the plan was "outdated" and citing constituent fatigue with multifamily ("too much multifamily development in Meridian" — Cavener). Category: **developer pivot to for-sale, enabled by a council-level comp-plan override** — the mirror image of Overland & Wells II (where Council enforced comp-plan/employment-land protections to kill an MF project) and of Newkirk (where the developer itself abandoned an *already-approved* 216-unit apartment component in March 2025 because, per the Statesman, it "yet to find a partner to build and manage" the units, calling standalone apartments there "economically unviable" without 400+ units' worth of amenity scale — a quote worth folding into the existing Newkirk graveyard entry, sourced to Rose Evans/Nick Rosenberger, Idaho Statesman, March 20, 2025, quoting deputy community development director **Caleb Hood** that Meridian multifamily permits fell from 968 units in 2022 to 175 in 2024).

**Opposition:** None documented from neighbors/public in the sources found — this was an intramural staff/P&Z-vs-Council split, not a citizen turnout fight. The "opposition" that mattered was internal: city planning staff and the Planning & Zoning Commission itself, both formally recommending denial.

**Land today:** Approved for 256 SF homes as of March 2024; build status UNVERIFIED as of Aug 2026. Whether or not it ever breaks ground, its practical effect already happened: roughly 35 corridor acres that the city's own specific area plan earmarked for apartments/townhomes/commercial is now zoned and platted for single-family only, permanently removing that increment from the corridor's MF pipeline.

**IC narrative:** In March 2024, Meridian's own City Council — over the express objection of planning staff and its Planning & Zoning Commission — voted 3-2 to let a for-sale developer build 256 single-family homes on 35 acres the city's 2007 Ten Mile Interchange plan had reserved for apartments and townhomes, with one councilmember stating on the record that there is "too much multifamily development in Meridian." Combined with the same-corridor collapse of the Newkirk apartment component in 2025 (developer: "economically unviable," unable to find an operating partner after two years) and the Meridian OZ Apartments denial/downsizing cycle on Franklin Road, this is a third, distinct data point that Meridian's political appetite for new corridor apartments is narrowing even where the underlying zoning or comp plan already supports it — a incrementally bullish signal for the subject's existing rent roll, though it says nothing about the large, already-entitled pipeline (Gateway at Ten Mile 390u, Outer Banks 516u, Vanguard Village 552u, The District at Ten Mile ~1,800u) that is moving forward regardless.
