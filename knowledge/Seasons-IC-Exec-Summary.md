# Seasons at Meridian — Executive Summary: Strengths & Considerations

**360 units | 2024 vintage | I-84 & Eagle Rd, SE Meridian, ID | Seller: Carlyle (CBRE) | First-round bids 8/19/26**
**Model (v3): recommended bid $122.0M ($338.9K/u, $363/SF) vs $125M whisper | Y1 cap 4.76% / T3 4.53% | 4-yr hold, exit Oct-2030 @ 5.00% = $144.7M ($401.9K/u) | UIRR 8.75% / LIRR 11.91% / Investor 9.42% | Debt $76.7M, 62.9% LTV, 5.40% fixed, full-term IO**
*Synthesized 2026-08-16 from the v3 model, the repo knowledge base, the parcel-level land-use work, and the Economy & Demand IC deck.*

## Strengths

- **Below replacement value, with the claim calibrated to survive IC.** ~$339K/u for 2024 product that costs ~$305K/u to build today but **$355–400K/u to build-and-exit** (two sponsors' own books); −8% vs Emblem's untrended stabilized value; **~23% below the jurisdiction-adjusted Canyon Ridge award** ($427.6K/u Meridian-equivalent). *Never say "below replacement cost" — we are ~8% above it, and anyone holding the dev books can check.*
- **The supply moat is economic, and it's shut.** At Seasons rent levels, new construction yields ~6.0% untrended vs 5.25–5.5% exit caps — no development margin. Our UW rents sit **11–14% below the 6.5% feasibility pencil** (normalized) and never cross even the 6.0% line during the hold. Two independent sponsors triangulate the same $214–216/NRSF hard cost.
- **Near-term supply is thin and dev capital is expensive.** Only 661u UC in 5 mi (448 Y1 / 213 Y2, zero after); Meridian pipeline down ~50% from 2023, zero 2025 deliveries; sponsors need 9–10% prefs and 20%+ LP IRRs at a true ~6.0–6.3% ROC — some marketed pipeline won't raise.
- **Structural Meridian tax advantage, verified at the county.** 0.45% effective levy vs Boise 0.92% (school district driven) ≈ $2,088/u/yr ≈ **~$42K/unit of value** at a 5% cap. Model taxes are conservatively set (98% reassessment step-up vs the 95% convention).
- **Y1 revenue requires no new asking growth.** Today's T90 asking ($1,939) already exceeds the first two UW quarters ($1,875/$1,855); Y1 avg $1,915 sits between the executed L5 ($1,885) and market. Concessions: **0 of the last 49 leases**, vs 67 of 67 a year ago. Lease-up is finished (63.7% → 96.1% occupied in 13 months).
- **Embedded loss-to-lease is a mechanical tailwind.** Contract $1,751 vs market $1,885 (−7.1%); burning it drives ~5.3% AGPR CAGR Y1–Y4 on only 4.0% market-rent growth.
- **The playbook is live-tested in our own portfolio.** Prelude (bought 12/25 at the market bottom, rocky takeover included): CY-26 landing within ~1% of UW, economic vacancy 11% → 6.1%, L5 +5.4% since close and **already above the CY-27 UW line**; trade-outs +6.5–6.6% at both assets vs the 3.5–4.0% carried in the UW.
- **Leading in-migration ZIP in the metro; site at the I-84/Eagle Rd interchange.** ZIP 83642 recorded
  the metro's highest net in-migration over the trailing year (+2,417, or +3.5% — 2.4× the metro rate),
  within an MSA that added 9,196 jobs (+2.4% YoY, QCEW) and 18.8K residents (+2.2%, the strongest year of
  the five-year panel). The location pairs employment at the doorstep with access to the rest: the site
  fronts the Eagle Rd commercial corridor and sits within Meridian's own employment base — St. Luke's
  (19 residents; 4.0% of the employed tenant base), Saint Alphonsus (13; ~2.7%), ICOM (4; ~0.8%), and the
  West Ada School District (4; ~0.8%) — while the I-84 interchange places the remainder of the metro's
  employment nodes, including downtown Boise and Micron (10 residents; 2.0%) at 11 mi, within a direct
  freeway commute. A >$2,000/mo ownership-cost premium favors continued rentership among the 51% of
  residents under age 30.
- **Tenant base is aligned with the metro's growth sectors, without single-employer dependence.** Per the
  seller's July 2026 demographics report (613 residents) and screened application data: median household
  income $89.3K (screened; ~44% above the $61.9K Meridian renter median), covering the $1,915 Y1 UW rent
  3.89× at the median; median age 27.5, 50% dual-earner households. The
  rent roll spans 366 distinct employers; the largest — St. Luke's, at 19 residents — represents 4.0% of
  the base, followed by Saint Alphonsus (13), Micron (10; 2.0% direct), Amazon (7), and a mix of
  government, education, and retail employers at ≤4 residents each. Healthcare and construction account
  for 19.3% and 13.0% of employed residents against 45% and 23% of metro job growth, with screened
  household incomes in those cohorts of ~$101K — the tenant base is drawn from the sectors driving the
  metro's employment expansion. The base is also strengthening as pricing normalizes: households that
  moved in during 2026 screen at a median income of **$96.6K, +11.8% above the standing pre-2026 base
  ($86.4K)**, with 49% above $100K (vs. 39%) — maintaining 4.2× coverage even against the $1,915 Y1 UW
  rent (n=94 of 275 income-reporting households; screened at move-in, like-for-like across cohorts).
- **Financing de-risks the hold.** 5-yr fixed at 5.40% (bought down), full-term IO, no floating exposure, no cap to repurchase; 62.9% LTV against a 3-month open window at exit.

## Considerations

- **Price and hold have moved — confirm the live basis.** The model now recommends **$122M on a 4-yr hold** (exit Oct-2030); the analytical record was built at $118M / 5-yr / 2031 exit. At $122M the Y1 cap compresses to 4.76% (5.00% at $118M) and entry-to-exit cap spread is only **24bp**.
- **Exit cap is the dominant exposure — and the anatomy is uncomfortable.** We assume aged product exits at 5.00% while the merchant book assumes 5.50% for brand-new; our exit NOI already carries a real ~9% vintage discount. **±25bp ≈ 80–95bp of UIRR**; at broker guidance (5.25%) LIRR ≈ 8.55%. Present the exit as a 5.00–5.25% band; the 5.25% case equals what 2-year-old Canyon Ridge fetches today.
- **The 4-yr hold exits into Emblem's sale window.** Quarterra is scheduled to sell 256 new units 1–2 mi away in Jan-2030; their $305K/u basis clears profitably at ~$340–350K — a potential comp print materially below our $402K/u exit assumption, now in the same year.
- **Rent growth is underwritten above consensus.** TMG Y2–Y4 at 4.0% vs vendor consensus 3.3–3.6% (CoStar ~2.1%); this market printed **−7.7%** from 3Q24 to 4Q25 before recovering — the downside is demonstrated, not hypothetical.
- **Occupancy is the unproven leg of Y1.** UW 94.5% vs 96.1% in place but 93.9% submarket; new leases trailed move-outs at both assets through peak season (49 vs 52; 23 vs 31); UW runs concessions to zero by Y4 while Prelude still runs 5.7% on new signings — on older product, but the assumption has no cushion.
- **The land optionality is real if economics flip.** 1,130 apartment-ready acres / 228 parcels within 5 mi (507 ac by-right; 309 ac within 2 mi), held by the valley's best-capitalized developers (Brighton, BVA, Hawkins, Quarterra, CBH); 4,494u proposed. **Emblem (250u, 1.0 mi, plans filed Jun-26) sits in the Bear scenario only** — the base case assumes zero proposed units deliver in the hold. The moat is also self-limiting: if rents beat UW, the pencil closes early and the roster converts (24+ mo lag), truncating late-hold upside.
- **One correlated macro bet, levered to soft data.** ~640 Meridian units post-close; every book in the stack needs rents +3.5–4%, caps 5.0–5.5%, muted supply, Micron-led demand. The demand deck's migration engine rests on Placer.ai device-panel data; the state-government jobs line (21% of Q4 growth) has already flipped negative; construction (23% of growth) is substantially the Micron build-out, which rolls off after '26.
- **Credit metrics are thin on our own numbers.** DSCR 1.11× T3 / 1.17× Y1 on TMG NOI — the $76.7M loan sizes only against CBRE's higher lender NOI; Y1 debt yield 7.6%. No value-add lever exists in the model (VA capex $0): returns rest entirely on market growth, LTL burn, and exit cap.
- **Model hygiene before IC.** Purchase-price sensitivity grids are stale (axis from another deal, values duplicated from the rent grid); the two DD NOI-bridge tabs are empty; One Pager says 5.30%/2.0% financing vs the Financing tab's 5.40%/2.6%. DD narrative reports no material issues, but the checklist itself is unfilled.

**Bottom line:** the buy case is basis + moat + a Y1 already signed — paying ~9% below the newest jurisdiction-adjusted comp for cash flows that require no new asking growth in year one, behind a development pencil that keeps competitors out at these rents. The bear case is a single correlated bet — rents, caps, and Micron — expressed at a 24bp entry-to-exit spread, with the largest single lever (exit cap on aged product) assumed 50bp friendlier than the nearest merchant seller's own book, in the same exit window.
