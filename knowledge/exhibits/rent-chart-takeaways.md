## Short version — for the chart's takeaways box

- **Both assets have round-tripped a full cycle and are at new highs.** Seasons T90 asking bottomed at
  $1,677 (Dec-25) and is $1,939 today — **+15.7% in eight months, +7.2% YoY**. Prelude bottomed at $1,629
  (Mar-26) and is $1,793 — **+10.0% off the trough, +3.9% YoY**.
- **Seasons has finished the transition from lease-up to stabilized pricing.** Effective rent went
  $1,420 (Nov-25) → $1,939 today, **+36.6%**, as concessions burned off: **zero of the last 49 leases
  (trailing 90 days) carried a concession, vs 67 of 67 a year ago.** Asking now equals effective.
- **The underwriting picks up *below* today's market.** The Seasons Y1 UW average of $1,915 (4Q26–3Q27)
  is **1.3% below** today's $1,939 T90 and only **1.6% above** the $1,885 L5 executed new-lease spot.
  Y1 asks for no new asking-rent growth — only that we don't give back what's already signed.
- **Prelude is the live proof of the same thesis in our own portfolio.** Executed new leases went
  **$1,717.50 at closing (12/3/25) → $1,810.62 (8/13/26), +5.4% in eight months** — already **2.0% above
  the model's 2027 market rent** and 2.7% ahead of the Q3-26 UW point.
- **What to watch: the winter.** The UW carries Seasons down only **4.3%** from today's peak to 1Q27
  ($1,855). Each of the last two winters ran **−6% to −7%** (Aug → 1Q), which off today's $1,939 implies
  **$1,800–$1,820**. Same test at Prelude: it must average **~$1,783 Aug–Dec** to make its CY-26 UW
  average of $1,730 — it sits at $1,793 today, with seasonal fade typically starting in September.

## Supporting detail (if you want a longer narrative version)

**1. The 2024–25 decline was lease-up and market softness, not a rent-level reset.** Seasons' 2024
asking series is lease-up pricing: the asking-to-effective spread ran **12–15%** through 2H-24 and again
in 4Q-25. It is **0.0%** in July and August 2026 across 49 leases. The asset is now pricing as stabilized
product for the first time.

**2. The two series tell different stories about *why* they recovered.** Seasons' recovery is
concession-led (effective +36.6% vs asking +15.7% off their respective troughs). Prelude's is
level-led — its concession spread has been ≤2.5% for most of the last 12 months, so its asking and
effective lines move together and the +10.0% off the March trough is real rent, not discount burn-off.

**3. Where the UW sits relative to what's already executed.**

| Anchor | Seasons | Prelude |
|---|---|---|
| T90 asking, 8/13/26 | $1,939 | $1,793 |
| T90 effective, 8/13/26 | $1,939 | $1,774 |
| L5 executed new lease | $1,885 (8/4/26 RR) | $1,811 (8/13/26 RR) |
| UW Y1 market-rent average | **$1,915** (4Q26–3Q27) | **$1,730** (CY 2026) |
| UW Y1 vs L5 executed | +1.6% | −4.4% (UW set pre-close) |

At Seasons, executed new leases run ~2.8% below the HelloData asking series — so the $1,885 L5 marker is
the conservative anchor and the $1,915 Y1 UW sits between the two observable marks, not above both.

**4. Prelude entered 2026 behind its UW and is exiting it ahead.** CY-26 asking has averaged $1,692
Jan–Jul vs the $1,730 UW year-average (−2.2%), because the model didn't underwrite the Feb–Mar dip. But
the current spot is 2.7% *above* the Q3-26 UW point, and the trajectory — not the year-average — is what
carries into Y2. The gap is a shape difference, not a level miss. (In-place rent is separately tracking
+0.7% ahead of the $1,668 UW rent-per-occupied-unit path.)

**5. The vintage spread is holding.** Seasons commands **+8.2%** over Prelude today (2024 vs 2018
vintage; SE Meridian vs Paramount). The UW holds that spread at +10.7% in Y1 and narrows it to +8.5% by
2031 — i.e., we are not underwriting Seasons to pull away from the submarket.

**6. Read-through to the Seasons Y1 revenue bridge.** Per the deal knowledge base, the Y1 bet is
(a) averaging today's executed asking across Nov-26–Oct-27, (b) concessions burning to ~1.5–3.0% of AGPR,
and (c) 94.5% occupancy. This chart directly de-risks (a) and (b): asking is above the Y1 average today,
and concessions are already at zero on new signings. Prelude's caution flag from the T12 work — new-lease
concessions still running ~5.7% of potential there — is a 2018-vintage phenomenon; the 2024-vintage
Seasons lease file shows none.

## Caveats to keep visible

- T90 is a **trailing-90-day** window, so the August points are July-weighted peak-season signings; they
  are not a full-year run rate.
- The UW lines are **period averages** (quarterly through Y1, annual at UW-year midpoints thereafter),
  plotted against monthly spot actuals. Do not read a single crossing point as "ahead" or "behind."
- Sample sizes in a 90-day window are 36–67 leases per property — enough to trend, thin enough that any
  single month can move $20–30.
- Seasons' pre-2026 asking is lease-up pricing from a 2024 delivery; comparisons to that period measure a
  lease-up-to-stabilized transition, not pure market rent growth.
